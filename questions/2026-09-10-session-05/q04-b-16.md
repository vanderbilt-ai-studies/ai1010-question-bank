**Question:** Why can adding many unrelated responsibilities make a skill harder to select?

- A. Every included responsibility makes the skill equally suitable for each task
- B. A broader skill can be selected without considering its description
- C. More responsibilities reduce the importance of the requested outcome
- D. Unrelated responsibilities make the skill's intended use less distinct

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Listing many kinds of work does not establish equal suitability. Unrelated responsibilities can make its intended use less clear.
- B. Not quite. The description still supplies the initial selection guidance. Breadth does not remove the need to assess relevance.
- C. Not quite. The requested outcome remains the basis for choosing a procedure. A broad collection of instructions cannot replace that match.
- D. Correct. A focused procedure is easier to match to a concrete task. Unrelated responsibilities blur the boundary of what the skill handles.
