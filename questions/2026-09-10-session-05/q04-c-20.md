**Question:** Why should a support repair record include what changed and information needed for reversal?

- A. Recording a change makes every attempted repair automatically reversible.
- B. A change record replaces the need to inspect the repaired object.
- C. The record lets the agent apply unfamiliar repairs without escalation.
- D. It supports tracing and undoing the repair where possible.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. A record supports reversal where possible but does not guarantee it. The workflow must preserve information relevant to the actual change.
- B. Not quite. Logging and verification serve different purposes. The record documents the action; inspecting the object checks whether it worked.
- C. Not quite. Documentation does not turn an unknown procedure into a known repair. Unfamiliar or ambiguous cases still need escalation.
- D. Correct. Recording the change and relevant prior information makes the intervention easier to inspect and reverse. It also preserves useful project state beyond the conversation.
