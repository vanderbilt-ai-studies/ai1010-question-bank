**Question:** Why must you evaluate a cheaper execution model even if a more capable model helped write the skill?

- A. Authoring quality does not establish execution quality
- B. The cheaper model should be rejected because another model wrote its instructions
- C. A stronger authoring model automatically installs all execution dependencies
- D. The authoring model permanently transfers its own abilities into the cheaper model

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. A well-developed procedure may help a cheaper model, but only tested results show whether it meets your requirements.
- B. Not quite. The proposed strategy may work. Evaluation determines adequacy rather than assuming failure from model differences.
- C. Not quite. Developing instructions does not establish that required software or access exists in the execution environment.
- D. Not quite. A skill supplies guidance; it does not transfer the authoring model's underlying capabilities into another model.

</details>
