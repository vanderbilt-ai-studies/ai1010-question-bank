**Question:** Why can arithmetic on learned word vectors sometimes capture a relationship between concepts?

- A. Relationships can be reflected in directions within the vector space.
- B. Vocabulary identifiers are arranged as a ranked list of definitions.
- C. Each vector contains a written rule for the requested word analogy.
- D. Temperature performs concept arithmetic before embeddings are assigned.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. A learned geometry can encode useful relationships. An analogy is an approximate pattern, not an exact identity guaranteed for every model.
- B. Not quite. Token identifiers identify vocabulary entries. Semantic geometry belongs to the learned vectors, not to a ranking of dictionary definitions.
- C. Not quite. Vectors are numerical representations rather than text instructions. Useful analogies can emerge from the learned arrangement.
- D. Not quite. Temperature affects output sampling later in the pipeline. It does not create the relationships in the embedding space.
