**Question:** Why would a transcript containing an important promise near its end be useful for testing a notes skill?

- A. It lets you use a longer generated answer as evidence of better coverage
- B. It tests whether the procedure preserves an easily missed requirement
- C. It makes repeated runs of this transcript sufficient to assess varied inputs
- D. It lets you judge completeness from the output without reopening the transcript

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Length does not establish that the important promise was captured. Inspect the output against the relevant source requirement.
- B. Correct. A difficult case can expose omissions that a straightforward transcript would fail to reveal.
- C. Not quite. Repeated success on this case provides evidence for that case. Representative inputs are still needed to assess other challenges.
- D. Not quite. An output can sound complete while omitting a promise. Compare it with the source to check the relevant requirement.
