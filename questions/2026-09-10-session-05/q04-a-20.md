**Question:** Why do successful recurrent-depth experiments leave the benefit of extra passes on a different model uncertain?

- A. Matching parameter counts is enough to transfer the measured improvement.
- B. Reusing fixed weights makes additional passes equivalent to a single pass.
- C. The length of a printed explanation determines the useful number of passes.
- D. The benefit depends on the model, its training, and the task.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Parameter count describes learned numerical values, not whether another model was trained to benefit from recurrent processing. The tested architecture and task matter.
- B. Not quite. Fixed weights can act on changing hidden representations. Reuse permits extra computation, but its benefit still needs evidence.
- C. Not quite. Visible text is an incomplete measure of hidden numerical computation. It cannot establish how much recurrent processing helps a different model.
- D. Correct. The research demonstrates a trained approach, not a general improvement from adding loops to an arbitrary model. Evaluate the amount of processing that helps in the intended setting.
