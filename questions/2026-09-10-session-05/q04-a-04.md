**Question:** What does an MLP (multi-layer perceptron) do inside a decoder block?

- A. It picks the next output token from the final distribution.
- B. It assigns a vocabulary identifier to each input fragment.
- C. It applies learned transformations at each token position.
- D. It retrieves an exact source sentence for each requested fact.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Output selection is a later step. The multi-layer perceptron transforms representations used to compute that distribution.
- B. Not quite. Vocabulary identifiers are assigned during tokenization. The multi-layer perceptron acts on representations inside the model.
- C. Correct. The multi-layer perceptron uses trained weights to transform the representation at each position. Learned knowledge is distributed through the network.
- D. Not quite. The world-knowledge metaphor does not make this layer a literal database. A learned transformation can produce plausible output without finding a source sentence.

</details>
