**Question:** Why does an action log separate a proposal, an attempt, and a verified result?

- A. They establish different amounts of completion evidence.
- B. Each is an interchangeable description of a completed operation.
- C. A detailed proposal guarantees that the operation will execute properly.
- D. Recording an attempted operation removes the need for later inspection.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. A proposal describes intent, an attempt shows that an operation was tried, and a verified result shows the checked outcome. Keeping them distinct prevents an attempt from being reported as success.
- B. Not quite. These stages are not equivalent. A proposed or attempted repair can still fail to produce the required result.
- C. Not quite. A well-specified proposal can guide execution but cannot guarantee its result. The environment still has to be observed after acting.
- D. Not quite. A log of an attempt documents activity, not necessarily success. Verification needs evidence about the resulting object or behavior.
