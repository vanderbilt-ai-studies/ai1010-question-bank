**Question:** How should a bounded technical-support workflow handle an unfamiliar problem?

- A. Apply the closest familiar repair and inspect only if challenged.
- B. Try a newly generated repair because it includes an undo plan.
- C. Treat a familiar phrase in the report as proof of a known case.
- D. Escalate the case with the evidence collected.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Similar symptoms do not establish that a known repair fits. An unfamiliar case belongs at the escalation boundary.
- B. Not quite. Reversal information can help manage a change, but it does not make an unfamiliar repair a known, authorized procedure. The bounded workflow escalates unfamiliar cases.
- C. Not quite. Matching a phrase is weaker than inspecting the current state and comparing it with a documented case. Ambiguous cases should be escalated.
- D. Correct. The workflow is bounded by known, authorized repairs. Escalation preserves useful evidence while handling uncertainty explicitly.
