**Question:** Why do positive results for curated skills in a benchmark leave the value of your new skill uncertain?

- A. The benchmark makes polished instructions sufficient evidence of a useful new skill
- B. The benchmark lets you infer your skill's value from who helped write it
- C. Your procedure and task still require their own evaluation
- D. The benchmark lets you select a cheaper execution model without testing your task

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. A polished file does not establish performance. The benchmark supports evaluating guidance rather than judging it by appearance.
- B. Not quite. The notes treat skill quality as an evaluation question. Human authorship or artificial intelligence assistance does not establish your procedure's performance.
- C. Correct. Evidence from tested curated skills does not establish that every new skill improves results.
- D. Not quite. A cheaper model may execute a well-developed skill adequately, but you must evaluate the cost-quality tradeoff on your requirements.
