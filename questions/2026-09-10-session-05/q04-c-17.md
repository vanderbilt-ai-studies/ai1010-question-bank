**Question:** Why should you correct a remembered preference that came from a role-play example?

- A. The example may not represent your actual preference.
- B. A preference mentioned in role-play is evidence of a lasting personal choice.
- C. The stored version should take priority because it came from an earlier message.
- D. The remembered preference can be treated as a record of completed project work.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. A system can mistake an imagined role for a fact about you. Inspecting and correcting that memory prevents later work from inheriting the mistaken preference.
- B. Not quite. An example can describe an imagined role rather than you. Its appearance in conversation does not establish a recurring personal preference.
- C. Not quite. Earlier context can be misinterpreted. Inspecting and correcting the memory helps it represent your actual preference.
- D. Not quite. A preference guides recurring choices; project state tracks changing facts and operations. Neither role turns a fictional example into a fact about you.
