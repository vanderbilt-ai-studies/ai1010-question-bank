**Question:** After a long conversation is summarized, you need to resume a project, but the summary omits whether a saved file passed its final check. What should you do?

- A. Assume the check passed because the summary says the file was saved.
- B. Reopen the project record and evidence to establish the check status.
- C. Use the earlier plan to validate the file as evidence that validation happened.
- D. Ask the agent for a confident completion statement based on the summary.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Saving and verifying are separate events. A missing check should remain unresolved until you recover or obtain evidence.
- B. Correct. Explicit state and accessible originals help recover details omitted by compaction. Use them to determine what remains before claiming completion.
- C. Not quite. A plan records an intended check, not its execution or result. Recover the check evidence or inspect the artifact before claiming completion.
- D. Not quite. The summary omits the needed evidence. Another generated statement cannot establish whether the file passed its check.
