**Question:** Which operation most directly describes attention in the decoder pipeline?

- A. Combining information from relevant context positions.
- B. Selecting one output token from its final probabilities.
- C. Increasing the number of learned weights during a reply.
- D. Replacing input tokens with their vocabulary identifiers.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Attention lets a representation incorporate information from permitted positions in the context. This helps account for how a token is being used.
- B. Not quite. Selecting the output token happens after the decoder blocks produce the next-token distribution.
- C. Not quite. Using attention during ordinary generation applies trained weights; it does not add parameters as the reply grows.
- D. Not quite. That is token encoding. Attention operates on numerical representations after the initial encoding and embedding steps.

</details>
