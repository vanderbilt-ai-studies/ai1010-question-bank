**Question:** Why can two skills with vague, overlapping descriptions lead to poor selection?

- A. Their descriptions fail to distinguish the intended uses
- B. Their latest modification dates determine which procedure fits
- C. Their overlapping words prove the procedures are interchangeable
- D. Their shared vocabulary makes the full instructions unnecessary

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Selection begins with compact guidance about relevance. Similar vague descriptions can make different procedures appear interchangeable.
- B. Not quite. Modification dates do not establish task fit. Clear differences in purpose and applicability are more useful selection evidence.
- C. Not quite. Similar wording can hide different procedures. The overlap creates ambiguity rather than proving the skills do the same work.
- D. Not quite. A description supports initial selection. Overlapping vocabulary does not replace the detailed instructions for carrying out the work.

</details>
