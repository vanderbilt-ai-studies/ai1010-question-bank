**Question:** A token has a 30% probability in the sampling distribution, while another has 60%. The sampler chooses the 30% token. What does that show?

- A. A sample can select an option other than the most probable one.
- B. The model must have changed its weights during the selection step.
- C. The 30% token must be more factually accurate than the 60% token.
- D. The model has increased its maximum available context window.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Thirty percent is a nonzero chance of selection. Sampling does not require picking the highest-probability token on every step.
- B. Not quite. The distribution can remain unchanged while different samples are drawn. A less probable selection is not evidence of a weight update.
- C. Not quite. Sampling probability is not a guarantee or ranking of factual correctness. The selected token still contributes to a claim that may need checking.
- D. Not quite. Selecting a token does not expand the system's context capacity. It extends the current sequence within that capacity.
