**Question:** Why can a fluent model response contain an incorrect factual claim?

- A. A false answer shows the model failed to select its highest-probability token.
- B. Sampling replaces learned probabilities with unrelated random words.
- C. Predicting plausible text differs from checking a claim's truth.
- D. A false answer means the relevant fact was absent from training.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. A high-probability continuation can itself contain an error. Selecting the most probable text does not verify its factual claims.
- B. Not quite. Sampling uses a distribution related to the model's scores. A false claim can still be a plausible continuation under that distribution.
- C. Correct. Next-token prediction can support fluent and useful answers, but fluency is not a verification step. Check important factual claims against evidence.
- D. Not quite. Learned representations can be imperfect, and generation is not a factual guarantee. An error alone does not show that the fact was missing from training.
