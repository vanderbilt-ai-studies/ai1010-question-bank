**Question:** Why is a plausible document title insufficient evidence that a repaired link is correct?

- A. The saved title is sufficient evidence that the link has the intended target.
- B. The agent's completion claim establishes that the destination was inspected.
- C. Opening a document establishes that the link now reaches the intended reading.
- D. Different documents can appear under the same convincing title.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. A plausible label can point to the wrong document. Reading the destination checks its identity rather than relying on the displayed label.
- B. Not quite. A completion sentence is not evidence that inspection occurred. Check the destination content and the actual action evidence.
- C. Not quite. A successful click establishes that a destination opens. You still need to inspect whether that destination contains the intended reading.
- D. Correct. A label can look appropriate even when the destination is wrong. Open the saved link and confirm that the document itself is the intended one.
