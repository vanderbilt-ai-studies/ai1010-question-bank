**Question:** What trade-off does the lecture identify as coming with looping?

- A. Looping leaves the model unable to generate any text.
- B. Looping removes the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) from the pipeline entirely.
- C. Looping makes the model's embeddings incompatible with the rest of the pipeline.
- D. Interpretability gets murkier, since some refinement happens silently in the repeated pass.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Generation continues as normal. Looping only changes how much refining a representation gets before the pipeline moves on.
- B. Not quite. Looping re-runs the same attention-then-MLP stack; it does not take a component out. The trade-off the lecture named is about what you can see, not about what is present.
- C. Not quite. The looped output is the same kind of representation, just more settled. The rest of the pipeline consumes it exactly as before.
- D. Correct. Work done inside an extra pass never becomes a readable token, so the visible reasoning trace covers less of what the model actually did. That is the cost paid for the added stability.
