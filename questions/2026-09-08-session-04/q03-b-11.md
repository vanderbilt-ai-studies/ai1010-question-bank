**Question:** A new model matches average human performance on most everyday writing and reasoning tasks, but a commentator says 'that's not real AGI (artificial general intelligence), since it still can't do X' for some newly emphasized task. Which idea from the lecture best names what is happening?

- A. The commentator is applying the Levels of AGI framework correctly, since it defines AGI as needing to do X.
- B. The model must not actually be reaching average human performance if a commentator disagrees.
- C. Moving the goalposts: as capability thresholds are met, the informal definition of AGI shifts to demand something more.
- D. This shows AGI was defined correctly from the start and has never changed.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. The Levels of AGI framework grades performance and generality against behavior rather than fixing one new required task; this scenario is not an application of that framework.
- B. Not quite. Disagreement about labeling does not by itself mean the underlying performance claim is false; the lecture's point was about the definition shifting, not about the performance being disproven.
- C. Correct. This matches the lecture's explanation for why the AGI milestone passed quietly -- each time a bar is cleared, a new, harder bar gets treated as the real definition.
- D. Not quite. This is the opposite of the lecture's argument, which was that the definition has shifted over time rather than staying fixed.
