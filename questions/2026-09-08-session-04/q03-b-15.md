**Question:** In the pipeline diagram described in the lecture, what does the ellipsis ('...') between two decoder stacks represent?

- A. A pause where the model stops to check its work before continuing.
- B. A missing part of the diagram that was never finished.
- C. A stand-in for however many more decoder stacks the model actually has.
- D. The point where image tokens are converted into word tokens.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. That describes something closer to reasoning or looping, both of which are separate ideas. The ellipsis is a drawing convention, not a step the model takes.
- B. Not quite. The ellipsis is deliberate, not an unfinished drawing. It stands for content that is real but too repetitive to draw out.
- C. Correct. Only two stacks fit on the slide, but a real pipeline repeats the same attention-then-MLP (multi-layer perceptron) pattern many more times than a diagram can show.
- D. Not quite. No conversion between token types happens there. The ellipsis marks more repetitions of the same decoder stack pattern.
