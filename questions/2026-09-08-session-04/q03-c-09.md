**Question:** A classmate says the research papers cited alongside the lecture's multimodal discussion prove exactly how GPT works inside. What is the best correction?

- A. They are checkable examples of the general approach, not descriptions of GPT's internals
- B. They are the technical reports the model vendors published about their own systems
- C. They were assigned reading earlier in the course and are unrelated to the topic
- D. They show that GPT and Claude use identical architectures underneath

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The lecture is explicit that neither cited paper reveals the internals of GPT or Claude, which are undisclosed. Each one is offered as a real, independently verifiable example of the kind of approach -- joint training, or projection -- being discussed.
- B. Not quite. This is the misconception to repair. The cited work is independent research, and the lecture says plainly that the internals of these products are not public.
- C. Not quite. The papers are directly relevant, since they illustrate the two approaches under discussion. The trouble with the claim is not relevance; it is treating an example as proof of one product's internals.
- D. Not quite. This overstates in the other direction. The lecture treats the two styles of image handling as different approaches, and in neither case as a confirmed account of a specific product.
