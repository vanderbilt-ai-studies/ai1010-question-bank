**Question:** Why might a model's post-training on a strongly reasoning-oriented predecessor's traces make the model dependent on reasoning to sound like itself?

- A. Because reasoning traces permanently rewire which words the model's tokenizer can produce.
- B. Because reasoning traces cost more to generate, which the model was trained to avoid.
- C. Because the model absorbed its standard for a good answer from reasoning-heavy examples.
- D. Because a model can only imitate text it saw during pre-training, not post-training.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Tokenization is fixed independently of what post-training contains. The lecture's point was about the model's learned style and standard of quality, not about its vocabulary.
- B. Not quite. Compute cost is not the mechanism the lecture described. The explanation was about what behavior the model learned to imitate, not about efficiency.
- C. Correct. The model's sense of what a good response looks like was built on reasoning-heavy output, so skipping reasoning can mean skipping the behavior it learned to reproduce well. The lecture offered this as an interpretation, not a disclosed fact.
- D. Not quite. Post-training is exactly the stage that shapes how a model uses what pre-training gave it, including whether and how it reasons. That is where the lecture placed this effect.
