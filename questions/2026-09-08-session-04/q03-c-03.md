**Question:** The lecture connects native multimodal training to a model's strength not just at understanding images, but at generating them. What is the reasoning behind that connection?

- A. Native training gives the model a much larger vocabulary of words to draw on
- B. Projection-based models add images through a separate generator bolted on later
- C. Images share the same semantic space as words, so a picture is produced token by token
- D. Native training makes image output deterministic rather than randomly sampled

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Vocabulary size is not what the lecture points to here. The connection is about where images sit relative to words inside the model, not about how many words it knows.
- B. Not quite. The lecture describes projection as a way of adding image understanding after text training, and it does not explain generation quality by a bolted-on generator. The reasoning given is about where images live in the model's representation.
- C. Correct. Because a jointly trained model never separates image space from word space, making a picture is another case of drawing tokens from a vocabulary one at a time, with a little sampling randomness at each step -- the same process used for a sentence.
- D. Not quite. The lecture says the reverse: image generation carries the same temperature-like sampling randomness as text, which is also why re-editing the same picture drifts over several rounds.
