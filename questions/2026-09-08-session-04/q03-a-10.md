**Question:** The agent building seat-minder was told things like 'it has to run on Vercel, it needs a database like this,' rather than being handed every detail of the stack. What do the notes say this approach made possible?

- A. Testing the software was no longer necessary before it went live
- B. The agent was guaranteed never to make a design mistake
- C. The project could skip having a North Star problem statement
- D. Reaching a working solution faster than by knowing every technology first

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Refinement carried on over several days and the concurrency risk got specific attention, so nothing about this approach skipped testing.
- B. Not quite. A real concurrency risk still had to be called out separately, so requirement-level direction did not head off every possible mistake.
- C. Not quite. These requirements came after the North Star, as guidance layered on top of it rather than as a substitute for it.
- D. Correct. Giving requirements instead of specifying each technology got to a solution far faster, with a first working prototype coming together in about an hour.
