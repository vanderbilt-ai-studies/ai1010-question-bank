**Question:** The lecture used the phrase 'a second bite of the apple' to describe what?

- A. A student getting a second attempt at a quiz question.
- B. A representation passing through the same decoder stack again, becoming further refined while staying stable.
- C. A model being retrained from scratch on new data.
- D. Adding a second, completely separate model to check the first model's answer.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. This phrase was used in the lecture about the architecture, not about quiz retakes.
- B. Correct. This matches the lecture: the phrase describes looping, where sending a stack's output back through itself gives the representation another pass to become more refined without losing stability.
- C. Not quite. Retraining from scratch is a much larger and different process than the extra inference-time pass the phrase describes.
- D. Not quite. The lecture describes one stack processing the same representation again, not a second, separate model reviewing the first model's work.
