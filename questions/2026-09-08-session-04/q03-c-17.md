**Question:** What mechanism does the lecture point to for why an earlier graded submission can bias the grading of a later one in the same chat conversation?

- A. Attention -- everything said earlier in the conversation can influence what the model generates next
- B. Sampling randomness -- each grade differs slightly by chance
- C. Post-training -- the reinforcement-learning stage makes the model grade every submission identically
- D. The harness -- providing files and memory lets the agent treat every submission identically

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The lecture ties this directly to attention: because a model draws on everything earlier in the conversation when producing new output, an earlier grading can shift how a later submission comes out, for better or worse.
- B. Not quite. Random sampling would produce small, undirected variation, not a systematic bias toward harsher or more lenient grading based on what came right before. The lecture points to context carrying forward, not chance.
- C. Not quite. Post-training shapes the model once, before any conversation happens; it cannot explain why grading drifts within a single ongoing chat. The bias described comes from what is currently in the conversation, not from training.
- D. Not quite. This actually describes the fix, not the problem. A harness giving each submission a fresh subagent is what prevents this bias; it is not the cause of it.
