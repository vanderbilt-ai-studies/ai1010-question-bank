**Question:** The seat-minder project began with a 'North Star' problem statement. What did that statement describe?

- A. The exact database schema and table design the app would need
- B. The programming languages and hosting provider the build had to use
- C. How the app would compare with competing classroom check-in tools
- D. Knowing students' names, who will take a cold call, and their thinking

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. A schema is a technical detail. The North Star named the problem to be solved, and it came before any technical decision was made.
- B. Not quite. Those choices came later, and the agent made them from stated requirements. The North Star itself named no technology at all.
- C. Not quite. No competitor comparison appears in the notes. The starting point was a personal account of a problem, not market research.
- D. Correct. That is close to what was actually typed into the agent: a statement about students going unnoticed in a large class, tied to knowing names, knowing who is willing to be called on, and knowing what people are thinking before class begins.
