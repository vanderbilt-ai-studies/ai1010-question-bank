**Question:** How did seat-minder change the instructor's experience of cold-calling, according to the notes?

- A. It let him call on people by name and photo, knowing who opted in
- B. It let him skip cold-calling, with the app picking answers for students
- C. It let him grade students automatically from the answers they gave
- D. It let him limit cold-calling to only the top-performing students

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Check-in supplies the names and photos and the opt-in highlight supplies the willingness, so a cold call in a large room stops being a call on an unidentified student.
- B. Not quite. The app helps identify and choose who to call on; it never answers on a student's behalf.
- C. Not quite. Grading turns up later in the session as a separate demo with a rubric and an agent, not as anything seat-minder does.
- D. Not quite. The opt-in is about comfort with being called on, not about performance, and no grade data feeds the seating chart at all.
