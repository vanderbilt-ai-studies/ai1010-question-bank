**Question:** A professor pastes a lab report into a chat session and asks it to 'grade this,' without attaching a rubric or any assignment description. The reply comes back confident and well-formatted. What does the lecture warn is the danger here?

- A. The chat session will refuse to grade anything without more information
- B. The reply will look messy, making the missing rubric obvious right away
- C. The model has no idea what it is rating, and polished output can hide that
- D. The chat session will crash partway through generating the response

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. The demo shows the opposite: the session answered readily and confidently. The danger is precisely that it did not refuse, with nothing to go on.
- B. Not quite. The demo's point is the reverse. The output looked polished, which is what makes the problem easy to miss rather than obvious.
- C. Correct. With no rubric or assignment description, the model is drawing only on general training about what grading looks like -- and because the result still reads well, confidence is easy to mistake for correctness.
- D. Not quite. Nothing in the demo describes a technical failure. The danger is in the reliability of the content, not in the session failing to finish.
