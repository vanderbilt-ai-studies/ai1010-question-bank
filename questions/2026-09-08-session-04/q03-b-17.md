**Question:** As defined in the lecture, what does 'looping' mean in a looped transformer?

- A. Feeding a stack's output back into that same stack for extra passes.
- B. Running the whole model twice on two different prompts and averaging.
- C. Repeating the training process several times over the same dataset.
- D. Sending the final output token back to the very first decoder stack.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Looping re-runs a stack over its own output instead of always handing it forward to a new stack, which is what gives the representation a second pass to settle.
- B. Not quite. That is an ensembling trick applied across separate runs. Looping happens inside a single forward pass, on one representation.
- C. Not quite. That describes training epochs. Looping is an architectural choice that acts at inference, every time the model runs.
- D. Not quite. Looping re-runs one stack over its own output, rather than routing a finished token back to the start of the pipeline.

</details>
