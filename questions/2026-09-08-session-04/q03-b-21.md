**Question:** The lecture compares a model's reasoning trace to a person explaining how they solved a math problem. What point does this comparison make?

- A. A stated reasoning process is a good proxy for what happened, not a complete record.
- B. People and models describe their reasoning with complete accuracy.
- C. Only models give unreliable explanations of their reasoning; people are accurate.
- D. A person who cannot explain their reasoning is lying about how they solved it.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. People often describe solving a problem in a way their own errors contradict -- not lying, just without full access to their process. A model's reasoning trace has the same limit.
- B. Not quite. The comparison was drawn to make the opposite point: a stated explanation can diverge from what actually produced the answer.
- C. Not quite. People were used as the parallel case, not the contrast. The unreliability shows up on both sides.
- D. Not quite. The lecture rejected this reading directly. An explanation that does not match the errors made is not a lie; the person simply does not know their own process.
