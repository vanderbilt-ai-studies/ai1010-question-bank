**Question:** During class a student raises their hand in seat-minder, and the instructor taps that raised hand on the teaching-view seating chart. What does that show?

- A. That student's photo and name, plus photos and names of several neighboring seats
- B. A running transcript of everything the student has said in class so far
- C. The student's grades on previous quizzes and labs
- D. A confirmation that the student has been marked absent

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The notes describe exactly this: tapping a raised hand pulls up the student's photo and name along with several neighbors, useful for calling on that student or picking someone nearby.
- B. Not quite. No transcript feature is described in the teaching view. It surfaces photos, names, and neighboring seats, not a log of speech.
- C. Not quite. Seat-minder's teaching view is about check-in, cold-calling, and attendance, not gradebook data. No grade display is described in the notes.
- D. Not quite. This reverses the situation described: a raised hand means the student is present and checked in, and tapping it surfaces identifying information, not an absence marking.
