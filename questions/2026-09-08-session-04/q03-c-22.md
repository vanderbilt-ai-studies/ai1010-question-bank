**Question:** The lecture says a jointly-trained (native multimodal) model has a concept like 'royal' come with visual associations 'built in from the start.' What does 'from the start' refer to?

- A. The start of the conversation, before the user has typed a first message
- B. The start of training, when images and text were learned in the same process
- C. The start of the projection step that maps image features into the model's space
- D. The start of the model's reasoning trace, just before it produces an answer

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. This is about training, not about any one conversation. The 'start' being described comes long before a user ever chats with the model.
- B. Correct. This approach never separates learning about words from learning about images, so a concept and its visual associations are there together from the first training step rather than being added later.
- C. Not quite. A projection step belongs to the other approach, where vision is added after text training is already finished -- the opposite of 'from the start'.
- D. Not quite. A reasoning trace happens inside a single response and has nothing to do with how images and words were learned. The phrase refers to training.

</details>
