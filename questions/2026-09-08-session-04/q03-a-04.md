**Question:** Why does it matter that seat-minder began with a problem statement (the North Star) rather than a technical specification?

- A. Technical specifications are not allowed when you are directing an agent
- B. It fixed what the software had to accomplish before any implementation detail
- C. It was necessary because the instructor could not have written a specification
- D. It guaranteed that the finished software would ship without any bugs

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Nothing in the notes bans a technical spec. Implementation guidance, like the airline seat-reservation analogy, arrived later, layered on top of the North Star.
- B. Correct. The North Star was the thing the software actually had to do. Only after that came implementation guidance, and the agent picked the technologies from there.
- C. Not quite. Requirements and guidance were given confidently throughout the build. The point is sequencing the goal ahead of the details, not an inability to specify anything.
- D. Not quite. A real concurrency risk still had to be named and guarded against, so starting from a problem statement did not remove the correctness work.
