**Question:** Why does the lecture claim large language models have effectively become excellent bash-script authors, even though few people are fluent in bash?

- A. Because bash commands are simpler to write than ordinary English sentences
- B. Because they compose filtering and extraction steps across many files correctly
- C. Because bash was weighted heavily in every model's post-training reward signal
- D. Because bash scripting needs memorized syntax rather than real understanding

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. This is not the reasoning given, and it does not hold up -- bash has its own unfamiliar syntax, which is exactly why the lecture notes that few people are fluent in it.
- B. Correct. The lecture's point is about composition: stringing together whittling, filtering, and extraction operations across many files is what models turn out to be very good at, and it fills a gap most people never had reason to close.
- C. Not quite. The lecture makes no claim about a specific post-training recipe for bash. Its reasoning is about the kind of task bash scripting is, not about how the model was trained.
- D. Not quite. This undersells what is being described. Composing correct multi-step operations over files takes more than recalling syntax.
