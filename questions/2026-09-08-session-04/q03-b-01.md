**Question:** The lecture offered two explanations for why even a very capable model like Opus 5 resists having its thinking turned off. What was the first explanation?

- A. Its post-training likely leaned on reasoning traces from a strongly reasoning-oriented predecessor model.
- B. Its vendor documentation reportedly forbids disabling reasoning for legal reasons.
- C. Its attention mechanism probably cannot run without a reasoning step between tokens.
- D. Its pre-training data apparently excluded examples of answering without reasoning.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. This is the training-shapes-voice explanation: if a model learned its style and its standard for a good answer from another model's reasoning-heavy output, dropping reasoning can cost both the quality and the voice that training built in. The lecture offered this as the instructor's own reading, not a vendor disclosure.
- B. Not quite. No such documentation appears in the notes. Both explanations in the lecture were offered as the instructor's own reading of the situation rather than anything the model's maker had published.
- C. Not quite. Mechanically nothing about token-by-token generation changes when reasoning is on or off -- the model still produces tokens the same way it always does. Reasoning is a mode that post-training adds, not something attention requires.
- D. Not quite. This puts the explanation in the wrong stage. The lecture's point was about post-training -- what kind of reasoning traces shaped the model's later behavior -- not about what pre-training contained.
