**Question:** According to the addendum, a recurrent-depth model can spend extra computation at inference time. What does it avoid having to produce in order to spend it?

- A. A final answer.
- B. A new set of weights.
- C. A longer training run.
- D. Extra visible tokens.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. The answer still comes. The extra computation happens before it, refining a hidden representation rather than replacing the output.
- B. Not quite. The same shared block, with the same weights, is what gets unrolled again -- that reuse is the point. Nothing new is created at inference time.
- C. Not quite. The addendum places this extra computation at inference, when the model is answering a question, not back in training.
- D. Correct. The refinement happens in a latent representation, so the model can put more computation into a hard problem without writing out a longer reasoning trace to do it.
