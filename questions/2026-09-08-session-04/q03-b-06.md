**Question:** The lecture is careful to frame both explanations for why Opus 5 keeps reasoning on as something specific. What is that framing?

- A. A peer-reviewed finding published by an outside research lab.
- B. The instructor's own reading of the situation, not a disclosed vendor specification.
- C. A confirmed detail from the model's official system card.
- D. A guess a student offered during the lab, not the instructor's.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. No such publication appears in the notes. The explanations are presented as one person's reading of a model's behavior, which is a much weaker kind of evidence.
- B. Correct. The notes say this explicitly, and it matters for how much weight to put on the two explanations: they are informed guesses about why a model behaves as it does, not confirmed technical documentation from its maker.
- C. Not quite. The notes deny this level of certainty. Treating an interpretation as an official disclosure is exactly the overreach the framing was guarding against.
- D. Not quite. The lab is where the finding came from -- students could not turn thinking off on Opus 5 -- but the two explanations for it came from the instructor.
