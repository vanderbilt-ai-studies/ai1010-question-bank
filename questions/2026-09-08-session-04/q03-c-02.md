**Question:** The lecture describes an Anthropic-style approach to giving a model vision, distinct from GPT-style native multimodal training. Which of these best describes it?

- A. Images and text are trained together in one self-supervised process from the very first step
- B. The model is pretrained on text first, and a separate later step maps image features into the representation space the model already learned for words
- C. The model generates images the same way it generates words, as a sequence of sampled tokens
- D. The model matches an incoming image against a fixed dictionary of known images to find the closest caption

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. That is the GPT-style, native multimodal approach the lecture contrasts this one with. The Anthropic-style approach adds vision in a separate, later step instead.
- B. Correct. This is the projection-based approach: a vision encoder's features get mapped, through a projection layer, into a language model that was already fully trained on text before images ever entered the picture.
- C. Not quite. The lecture connects this image-as-tokens behavior to the native multimodal approach's strength at generation, not to the projection-based approach, which was described mainly in terms of understanding images.
- D. Not quite. That describes a lookup or retrieval system, not what either approach in the lecture does. Both methods build a learned representation of the image rather than matching it against a stored dictionary.
