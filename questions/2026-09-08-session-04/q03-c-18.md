**Question:** The demo mentions copying and pasting each submission into a brand-new chat, one at a time, as a way to avoid the context-bleeding problem from grading in one long conversation. Why does the lecture call this 'not terrifically repeatable'?

- A. Because each new chat costs significantly more money to run than one long conversation
- B. Because doing this by hand for well over a hundred submissions does not scale
- C. Because a brand-new chat cannot be given the rubric at all
- D. Because the course's learning platform does not allow more than one browser tab open at a time

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Cost is not the reason given. The concern raised is about the practicality of the manual process itself, not about price.
- B. Correct. The lecture notes the course has well over a hundred submissions at a time; manually starting a fresh chat and re-pasting the rubric for each one is possible but tedious enough that it is not a realistic routine at that scale.
- C. Not quite. A new chat can absolutely be given the rubric by pasting it in again -- that is exactly the manual step being described. The limitation is the effort of doing it by hand, not an inability to include the rubric.
- D. Not quite. This is not a constraint mentioned anywhere in the lecture. The repeatability problem is about manual effort at scale, not a platform restriction.
