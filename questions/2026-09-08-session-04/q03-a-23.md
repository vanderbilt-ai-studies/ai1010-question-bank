**Question:** As explained in the Lab 1 debrief, what does a non-reasoning model do when it responds to a question?

- A. It searches a database of previously answered questions for a match
- B. It picks a token from a probability distribution and commits right away
- C. It waits for a human to approve its draft before finalizing an answer
- D. It generates several full answers and lets the user pick the best one

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. That describes a lookup system. The model is still generating tokens rather than retrieving a stored answer, whether or not reasoning is switched on.
- B. Correct. There is no stepping back and no assessing: the answer starts immediately, one token at a time, along whatever path the first choice sets.
- C. Not quite. No approval step sits inside generation. The distinction being drawn is entirely about what the model does on its own before committing.
- D. Not quite. Generating and comparing candidate answers is close to what a reasoning model does privately, and it is never surfaced to you as a menu to choose from.

</details>
