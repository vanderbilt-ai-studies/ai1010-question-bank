**Question:** In the Lab 1 debrief, what task did a student use to compare a mid-size Claude model with reasoning turned off against the same model with reasoning turned on?

- A. Working out the day of the week for a given date
- B. Summarizing a long research paper down to a few paragraphs
- C. Translating a paragraph of text from English into Spanish
- D. Writing a short poem about the seat-minder check-in app

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Getting that right turns on fiddly details such as leap years, which is what made it a useful way to see whether reasoning changes the outcome.
- B. Not quite. Summarization is not the comparison described in the debrief; the task chosen had a single checkable right answer.
- C. Not quite. No translation task appears in the debrief. The comparison used a calculation whose answer could be marked right or wrong.
- D. Not quite. No creative-writing task is described. The point of the comparison was a task where a missed detail produces a clearly wrong answer.
