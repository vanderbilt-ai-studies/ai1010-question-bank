**Question:** What problem is Google DeepMind's Levels of AGI (artificial general intelligence) framework, mentioned in the lecture, designed to address?

- A. It provides a single yes-or-no test any model can be run through once.
- B. It replaces a single yes/no threshold with graded levels of performance and generality.
- C. It certifies which company may use the term AGI in its marketing.
- D. It counts a model's parameters to decide whether it qualifies as general.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. This is the opposite of the framework's purpose. A single pass/fail threshold is precisely the thing it was built to replace, because that threshold kept being redrawn.
- B. Correct. Grading performance and generality against what a model actually does gives you something checkable, instead of one label that gets redefined every time it is met.
- C. Not quite. The notes describe no certification or naming-rights function. The framework is a way of measuring capability, not of policing who may use a word.
- D. Not quite. Parameter count is not what the framework grades. It looks at levels of performance and generality in a model's actual behavior.
