**Question:** Before Agent 2.0-style systems, what did a company's 'agent' product typically look like, according to the lecture?

- A. A rigid, single-purpose pipeline hand-wired by a programmer to do one thing
- B. A general assistant that pulled in files, skills, and subagents as it went
- C. A chat window with a longer context window and no other change
- D. A model retrained from scratch for each customer's particular task

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The lecture calls this categorically different from what came later. An earlier agent had to be built by hand for one task, while an Agent 2.0 system pulls in files, skills, and subagents as it goes and can be pointed at many problems.
- B. Not quite. That is the Agent 2.0 description, which the lecture treats as the new thing rather than the old one. Earlier products could not assemble what they needed on the fly.
- C. Not quite. This is the 'chat with extra steps' reading the lecture rejects. Earlier agentic products were built pipelines, not a chat session with more room in it.
- D. Not quite. The rigidity described came from the hand-built pipeline around the model, not from retraining a model for every customer.
