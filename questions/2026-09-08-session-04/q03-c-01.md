**Question:** GPT-style models are trained on images using self-supervised learning, the same general method Session 3 introduced for text. What does that look like when applied to images, as described in the lecture?

- A. Images and text are folded into one shared sequence from the start, with pieces of that sequence left out for the model to predict
- B. A separate model is trained only on images, then combined with the text model afterward through a projection step
- C. Human labelers write captions for each image, and the model is trained to reproduce those captions
- D. An already-trained language model is simply shown static images afterward and asked to describe them, with no additional training involved

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. This is the native multimodal approach the lecture describes: text and images are part of the same training process from day one, treated as a shared sequence rather than two separate pipelines bolted together.
- B. Not quite. That describes the other approach the lecture contrasts this with -- the Anthropic-style method of pretraining on text first and adding vision later. It is a real approach, just not this one.
- C. Not quite. Writing captions for supervision is a plausible-sounding training method, but it is ordinary supervised learning with human labels, not the self-supervised, predict-the-missing-piece method the lecture describes.
- D. Not quite. That describes using a finished model at inference time, not training it. Both approaches the lecture discusses involve real training on image data, whether jointly from the start or in a later step.
