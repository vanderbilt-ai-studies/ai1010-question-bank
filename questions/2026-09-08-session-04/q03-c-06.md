**Question:** Why might a very large, high-resolution monitor actually slow down a computer-use agent, according to the practical tip mentioned in class?

- A. A bigger screen requires more physical mouse movement, which takes longer per click
- B. The agent has to interpret a bigger screenshot image at every step of its loop
- C. High-resolution displays are not supported by most screenshot-taking software
- D. The agent has to scroll further on a large screen to reach the controls it needs

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. The agent is not moving a physical mouse across real distance; it is choosing coordinates to act on. The slowdown described comes from processing the image, not from travel.
- B. Correct. The loop repeats a screenshot-then-decide cycle at every step, so a higher-resolution image simply costs more to interpret each time, and that adds up across a multi-step task.
- C. Not quite. This is a made-up compatibility issue, not the reason given in class. High-resolution screenshots work fine; they are just larger for the model to take in.
- D. Not quite. A larger screen usually means less scrolling, not more, and an agent can act on any coordinate it can already see. The cost that grows with screen size is interpreting a bigger image at every step.

</details>
