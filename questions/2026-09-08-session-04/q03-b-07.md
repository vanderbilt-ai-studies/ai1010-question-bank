**Question:** According to the lecture, what was the original working definition of AGI (artificial general intelligence) used across the field for decades?

- A. Outperforming the best human expert at every possible task.
- B. Passing a specific, standardized written exam designed for AI systems.
- C. Performing at the level of an average human on most tasks.
- D. Operating without human supervision for an extended period.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. This is a far higher bar than the definition the lecture gave, which asked only for average human performance -- and the gap between the two bars is exactly what the moving-goalposts discussion is about.
- B. Not quite. The lecture did not define AGI by a single test. The definition was about the level and breadth of task performance, not about clearing one exam.
- C. Correct. The original bar was deliberately modest: typical human performance, across most tasks rather than all of them. Holding that definition steady is what lets you notice when it has been cleared.
- D. Not quite. Autonomy over time is a different property. The definition in the notes is about how well and how broadly a system performs, not about how long it runs unattended.
