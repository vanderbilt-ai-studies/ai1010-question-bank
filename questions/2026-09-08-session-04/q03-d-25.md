**Question:** A classmate's version of the lecture-notes skill produces notes that read like a story and are not detailed enough to answer quiz questions built from the same lecture. Based on how the lecture addressed this exact complaint, what should the classmate do, and how can they check whether it worked?

- A. Switch to first-person narration and add screenshots, since detail comes from images
- B. Cut each section to a paragraph and stop checking the notes against the quiz
- C. Reword the skill toward grounded, expository notes, then answer a quiz from them
- D. Leave the wording alone, since a skill cannot control how detailed its output is

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Screenshots were an optional personalization choice, not the source of the missing conceptual detail, and person is a different axis altogether from narrative versus expository.
- B. Not quite. That addresses length, not the complaint, which was about missing grounding and detail. Dropping the quiz check also throws away the one diagnostic that was recommended.
- C. Correct. Ground the notes in the course's own content and references and explain directly rather than narrate, then check the fix by trying to answer the quiz from the notes alone. The rewording is the change; the quiz attempt is how you learn whether it worked.
- D. Not quite. The entire live-editing discussion was about fixing exactly this complaint through the skill's own wording, so treating the output as beyond the instructions' reach gets it backwards.
