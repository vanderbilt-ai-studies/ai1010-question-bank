**Question:** A student uses a top-tier reasoning model for a single quick factual question and is annoyed that it still 'thinks' for several seconds before answering, even though the question needs no multi-step work. Based on the lecture, which explanation best accounts for this?

- A. It is built mainly to work as an agent on long tasks, so reasoning stays on.
- B. It is deliberately slowed down so that people will not overuse it.
- C. Quick factual questions need more computation to answer than complex ones do.
- D. Looping forces every answer to pass through the decoder stack a second time.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. A model designed to sustain multi-step agent work keeps reasoning available even when a particular request is short and simple. The lecture offered this as its own reading of the design choice, not as a documented setting.
- B. Not quite. The notes describe no such throttling. The explanations given are about training and intended use, not about rate limiting.
- C. Not quite. This reverses the usual relationship, and nothing in the notes supports it. A short factual question does not inherently demand more computation than a hard one.
- D. Not quite. Looping is a separate architectural idea about refining a representation inside a stack. The lecture never offered it as the reason reasoning cannot be turned off.

</details>
