**Question:** Why does the lecture suggest the AGI (artificial general intelligence) milestone passed quietly, without much public agreement that it had happened?

- A. The definition of AGI itself shifts each time a capability threshold is reached.
- B. The companies that build these models suppress announcements about AGI.
- C. No model has reached average human performance on ordinary tasks.
- D. AGI was redefined as a legal term with no technical meaning.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. This is the moving-goalposts problem: the bar keeps being redrawn as models improve, so clearing the original bar never registered as a single, agreed-upon event.
- B. Not quite. The notes describe no concealment. The explanation offered is about the definition moving over time, not about anyone hiding a result.
- C. Not quite. This contradicts the argument in the notes, which was that average-human performance across most tasks had in fact been reached under the older definition.
- D. Not quite. The shifting described in the notes happens in how the field informally talks about capability thresholds, not in law or regulation.
