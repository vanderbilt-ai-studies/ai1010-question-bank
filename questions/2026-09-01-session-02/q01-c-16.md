**Question:** The lecture called the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) "a fuzzy JPEG of the world." What is the point of that comparison?

- A. Pictures of the things the model read about in its training text
- B. An exact, uncompressed copy of the training text, ready to be reproduced on demand
- C. A picture the model consults whenever it needs to check a fact
- D. A compressed, lossy picture of the world, sharp in places and blurry in others

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. The comparison is about compression, not images. A JPEG stands in for any lossy copy: it keeps the gist and loses detail, which is what happens to training text on its way into the weights.
- B. Not quite. An exact copy is what a JPEG is not. The whole reason to reach for the metaphor is that the MLP loses information, keeping some parts sharp and letting others blur.
- C. Not quite. Consulting would be a lookup, and the MLP does not do lookups. The JPEG image is about what the weights are like, a compressed and imperfect picture, not about how they are used.
- D. Correct. Like a JPEG, the MLP compresses an enormous amount of information and loses some of it. Topics heavily represented in training stay sharp; rare details blur.
