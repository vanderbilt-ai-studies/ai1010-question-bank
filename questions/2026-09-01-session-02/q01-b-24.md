**Question:** A student notices that in one model's vocabulary, "king" is token number 4,127 and "queen" is token number 4,128, and concludes that the model must know the two words are related. What is wrong with that reasoning?

- A. Nothing; consecutive numbers are how the model records related words
- B. Ids carry no meaning; relatedness shows up as nearby embeddings
- C. The model never uses those id numbers, so nothing can follow from them
- D. "King" and "queen" are not related in the model, so the conclusion is false

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Ids are handed out by the tokenizer without any regard for meaning. Two words could be neighbors by id and unrelated, or related and thousands of ids apart.
- B. Correct. The number is just a label used to look up the embedding. Meaning only appears once the id is mapped to a location in the semantic space, and there "king" and "queen" really are close.
- C. Not quite. The id does get used: it is the label the embedding is fetched by, so it is not idle. What it cannot do is carry meaning, which is why two neighboring ids tell you nothing about the two words.
- D. Not quite. The conclusion happens to be true -- "queen" sits right beside "king", along with "monarch" and "crown". It is the evidence that fails, because relatedness cannot be read off the id numbers.
