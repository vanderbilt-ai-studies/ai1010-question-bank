**Question:** Token encodings and token embeddings are both ways of turning a token into numbers. What is the difference between them?

- A. Encodings are used for common words and embeddings for rare ones
- B. An encoding is an arbitrary id from the vocabulary; an embedding is a location whose position carries meaning
- C. An encoding carries the token's meaning; an embedding is just a shorter code for it
- D. Encodings are computed by the decoder stacks; embeddings arrive already in the context

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Both stages apply to every token; there is no split by word frequency. Each token is first encoded, then embedded.
- B. Correct. The encoding is just a lookup number, like a seat number in a theater. The embedding places the token in a semantic space where nearby tokens mean similar things, so its position says something about what the token means.
- C. Not quite. This has the two stages backwards. The encoding comes first and is meaningless on its own; the embedding is the larger object, thousands of numbers, and it is where meaning lives.
- D. Not quite. Neither is produced by the stacks and neither arrives in the context. Both are early stages that prepare the text for the stacks to work on.

</details>
