**Question:** Why does the meaning of "king" need to be moved at all when the model reads "King me"?

- A. Because the embedding for "king" is random until attention assigns it a meaning of its own
- B. Because the model has never seen this exact sentence in its training text
- C. Because the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) has not yet had a chance to see the word
- D. Because the embedding is a first guess, made with no context, and that guess is royalty

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. The embedding is not random; it is a learned location in semantic space, and for "king" that location sits among queen, monarch, and crown. The problem is that this location is a first guess made without looking at any other word.
- B. Not quite. The model does not need to have met a sentence before; it works out meaning from the words that are present. What makes the move necessary is that the embedding for "king" was fixed before any of those words were read.
- C. Not quite. The MLP acts after attention, using what it learned in training, so it is not what makes the initial embedding a poor fit here. The embedding is off because it was chosen before any context was read.
- D. Correct. The token embedding places "king" among monarchs and crowns, since that is what "king" usually means. Attention exists precisely to correct first guesses like this one using the words around it.
