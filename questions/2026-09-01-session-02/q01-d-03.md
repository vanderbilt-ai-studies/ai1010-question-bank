**Question:** The lecture says that all the transformer does is produce a probability distribution over the tokens. What does that tell you about how the actual next word gets picked?

- A. Picking a token is a separate step that happens after the transformer has finished
- B. The transformer picks the word and then reports how confident it was
- C. The transformer writes several candidate sentences and keeps the best one
- D. The transformer checks the distribution against a table of facts before choosing

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The transformer's job ends at the distribution. Choosing one token from it, with a little noise added, is a step outside the transformer, and that separation is what makes temperature a setting you can change without touching the model.
- B. Not quite. It is easy to imagine the choice coming first and the probability being a confidence score attached to it. The order is the reverse: the probabilities come out of the transformer, and only then does something choose a token from them.
- C. Not quite. The model generates one token at a time, not whole sentences to compare. What it produces at each step is a spread of probabilities over single tokens, and one is chosen before the next step begins.
- D. Not quite. There is no table of facts anywhere in the pipeline. The distribution is the end of the transformer's work, and nothing downstream checks it for truth, which is exactly why a plausible answer and a correct one can differ.
