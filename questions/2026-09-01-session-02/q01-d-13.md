**Question:** How does the lecture reframe what people call a hallucination?

- A. As the model doing exactly what it always does, in a case where the context did not contain the answer
- B. As a rare fault in the decoder stacks that better engineering will eventually remove
- C. As what happens when the model's fact database returns the wrong entry
- D. As a sign that the model was trained on false information

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The same process produces every answer, right or wrong: a probable continuation drawn from the context and the fuzzy JPEG. When the context lacks the answer, the JPEG fills in something plausible, and that is what gets labeled a hallucination.
- B. Not quite. That treats hallucination as a bug, which is what the lecture pushes back on. The stacks are working normally when the model is wrong; they are producing a plausible continuation, and no fix to the mechanism changes what it selects for.
- C. Not quite. There is no fact database to return anything. The MLP (multi-layer perceptron) is a lossy JPEG of the world, and a wrong answer is the blurry part of that picture being read off as if it were sharp.
- D. Not quite. A model can be wrong about something its training text got right, because the JPEG is fuzzy where a topic was rare. The wrongness comes from the mechanism producing something plausible without the answer in context, not from bad training data.
