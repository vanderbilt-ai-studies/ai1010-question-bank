**Question:** To build intuition for embeddings, the lecture asked how you would specify where you are standing in the classroom: distance from one wall, distance from another, and height. How does that picture scale up to a token embedding?

- A. The token gets thousands of coordinates rather than three
- B. The three numbers become the token's id, length, and frequency
- C. Each token gets three numbers, for grammar, meaning, and style
- D. Each number records the distance to one specific other token

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Three numbers pin down one spot in a room. A token embedding pins down one spot in a space with thousands of dimensions, and that spot is what the token means.
- B. Not quite. Those are facts about the token, not coordinates of meaning. The embedding's numbers are coordinates, and it takes thousands of them to place a word among its neighbors.
- C. Not quite. Three labeled dimensions would be far too few, and the dimensions are not hand-labeled. The space has thousands of dimensions, learned during training.
- D. Not quite. Dimensions are not distances to particular words. They are coordinates, like distance from a wall; distances between tokens are computed from those coordinates afterward.
