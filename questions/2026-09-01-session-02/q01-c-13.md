**Question:** What does the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) in a decoder stack contribute?

- A. What the model learned about the world during training
- B. A weighted average of the earlier words in the context
- C. The random noise that varies the model's answers
- D. The number assigned to each token in the vocabulary

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The MLP is where world knowledge lives: it is what knows that you king someone in checkers, not chess, and that "king me" is said when winning. It applies that knowledge by nudging the embedding attention handed it.
- B. Not quite. That is attention, the first half of the stack. The MLP takes attention's output and adds what the model already knew before it read this context.
- C. Not quite. Noise enters once, at the end, when one token is picked from the probabilities, and it is set by temperature. The MLP is deterministic and runs inside every stack.
- D. Not quite. Assigning numbers is token encoding, which happens before anything else. The MLP works far downstream, on embeddings that attention has already shaped.

</details>
