**Question:** How does a small language model relate to a large one, according to the lecture?

- A. The same architecture throughout, with fewer stacks and smaller layers inside them
- B. A simpler architecture that leaves out the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) layers to save space
- C. The same model with its vocabulary cut down to the most common words
- D. A large model with most of its decoder stacks skipped at run time to save memory

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. A small model has the same attention-plus-MLP (multi-layer perceptron) stacks, just fewer of them and smaller. Its JPEG of the world is blurrier, and the trade is that it fits on hardware you carry, such as a laptop or a phone.
- B. Not quite. Dropping the MLP would drop the model's world knowledge entirely, and no model does that. Small models keep every part of the architecture; each part is just smaller.
- C. Not quite. The vocabulary is not where the size lives. What shrinks in a small model is the parameter count in the MLPs (multi-layer perceptrons) and the number of decoder stacks.
- D. Not quite. A small model is built and trained small; it is not a large one with parts switched off mid-run. It has fewer stacks and smaller MLPs (multi-layer perceptrons) from the start.
