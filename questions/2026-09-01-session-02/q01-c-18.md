**Question:** A model explains the rules of checkers accurately but gets the details of a specific nineteenth-century championship match wrong. Which idea from the lecture explains this?

- A. Attention failed to weight the right words in the prompt
- B. The MLP (multi-layer perceptron) is lossy: common topics sharp, rare details blurry
- C. The entry for that match in the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) was corrupted or lost
- D. The temperature was set too high for factual questions

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. If the match details were in the prompt, attention would pull them in. The trouble here is that they were never in the context, so the model had to fall back on what its weights hold, and for this match that is not much.
- B. Correct. The rules of checkers appear all over the training text, so they are rendered sharply in the weights. One old match barely appears, so the weights hold only a blur, and the model fills it in plausibly rather than accurately.
- C. Not quite. There are no entries in the MLP to corrupt; it is not a database with a row per fact. The detail was never stored crisply in the first place, because it was too rare in training to leave a sharp trace.
- D. Not quite. Temperature adds a little noise to the final choice, but it cannot invent knowledge that is not there or destroy knowledge that is. The gap here is in what the weights hold.
