**Question:** The class played the model's game with the opening line of Zora Neale Hurston's novel: "Ships at a distance have every man's wish on ____." Which three kinds of knowledge did the lecture say you needed just to play?

- A. Spelling, punctuation, and a large vocabulary
- B. Syntax, semantics, and style with world knowledge
- C. The author's biography, the novel's title, and its publication year
- D. Syntax, semantics, and a memory of having read the novel

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. None of these were the point. You could have guessed a noun with a modest vocabulary; what mattered was knowing what kind of word fits, what the sentence is about, and how a writer like Hurston would finish it.
- B. Correct. You knew the slot needed a noun (syntax), that the noun should concern ships and wishing (semantics), and that 'board' is the poetic choice a novelist would make (style and world knowledge). A model has to learn all three to guess well.
- C. Not quite. Knowing about Hurston helps with the last step, but the game was not trivia. Nobody needed the title or the year to know that a noun about ships belonged there.
- D. Not quite. The first two are right, and the third is the tempting swap. You did not need to have read Hurston to play; you needed a sense of how writers choose words and of the world the sentence describes, which is the third thing the model has to learn.
