**Question:** For the context "She looked across the board and said triumphantly, 'King ...'", which description of the resulting token probabilities is right?

- A. "me" gets the highest probability and "cat" gets a moderate one, since it is a common noun
- B. "checkers" gets the highest probability and "cat" gets zero
- C. "me" gets the highest probability and "cat" is dropped from the vocabulary
- D. "me" gets the highest probability and "cat" gets essentially zero

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Overall word frequency is not what decides the probabilities at this point. By the last stack the representation carries that "king" is a verb in checkers with a winner speaking, and "cat" fits none of that, so its probability is essentially zero, however common a word it is.
- B. Not quite. The model has worked out that the game is checkers, but that is not the same as predicting the word "checkers" next. "King" here is a transitive verb that needs an object, and the winner says "King me," so "me" is the token that wins.
- C. Not quite. The vocabulary is fixed; no token is ever removed from it during generation. "Cat" stays in the vocabulary and receives a probability like every other token; that probability is just essentially zero here.
- D. Correct. By the last position the representation knows the verb is transitive, the game is checkers, and the speaker is winning, so "me" dominates. "Cat" is still in the vocabulary and still gets a number, but that number is as close to zero as makes no difference.
