**Question:** What does the word "large" literally refer to in "large language model"?

- A. A larger and more carefully checked collection of training facts
- B. A longer context window than a small model has
- C. Bigger MLPs (multi-layer perceptrons) and more decoder stacks
- D. A more advanced architecture than a small model uses

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Large describes the model, not a curated set of facts; there is no fact collection inside it. The size is in the parameters: bigger MLPs (multi-layer perceptrons) and more decoder stacks.
- B. Not quite. Context length is a separate matter, and the lecture treats context as the other half of the picture, not as what large means. The slide shows only one change for a large model: the boxes get taller.
- C. Correct. That is the whole difference between the small-model slide and the large-model one: taller boxes and more of them. More parameters means more room for a sharper JPEG of the world, up to hundreds of billions of parameters in the largest models.
- D. Not quite. Small and large models use the identical architecture. The transformer introduced in 2017 is still the one in use; what changed is the scale, not the design.
