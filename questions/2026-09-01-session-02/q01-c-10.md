**Question:** A model reads "She finished her sales pitch and waited" in one conversation and "After a week of rain, the muddy pitch was unplayable" in another, and handles "pitch" correctly both times. Which idea from the lecture explains this?

- A. The model has two separate tokens, one for each of the two meanings of "pitch"
- B. The MLP (multi-layer perceptron) stores a definition for each meaning and selects one
- C. Attention lets the surrounding words move "pitch" to a different place in each sentence
- D. The model memorized both sentences during training

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. A token is just a number for a string of characters, so both sentences hand the model the same token. The difference arises afterwards, when the embedding is moved by context.
- B. Not quite. The MLP holds no definitions and does no selecting; it is weight matrices that nudge an embedding along. The sentence-by-sentence difference comes from attention, which reads the surrounding words.
- C. Correct. Attention averages in the tokens that came before, so "sales" pulls the embedding toward persuasion while "rain" and "muddy" pull it toward a playing field. Same starting embedding, different weighted average, different meaning.
- D. Not quite. Training teaches the model to guess the next word, not to store sentences. Even for a sentence it has never seen, attention can use the words present to settle what "pitch" means.
