**Question:** Why would a model that always took the single most likely token tend to produce flat, repetitive text?

- A. Because the most likely token is usually the wrong one
- B. Because the model would soon run out of tokens in its vocabulary
- C. Because the probabilities would drift and the model would lose the context
- D. Because the same common words and phrasings would keep coming out on top

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. The most likely token is a perfectly good guess most of the time; that is what makes it the most likely. The problem is not accuracy but monotony: always taking the top choice never explores the many other reasonable continuations.
- B. Not quite. The vocabulary never runs out; every token stays available at every step. The trouble with always taking the top choice is that the same well-worn words would keep winning, not that anything is used up.
- C. Not quite. The probabilities do not drift; the same context gives the same probabilities every time. Repetition comes from the choice rule, not from any loss of context.
- D. Correct. People repeat themselves, so the most ordinary wording sits at the top of the distribution again and again. Adding a little noise before choosing lets the model take a different path and explore, which is the whole reason for temperature.
