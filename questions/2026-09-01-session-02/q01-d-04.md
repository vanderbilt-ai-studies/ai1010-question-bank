**Question:** Given the context "She looked across the board and said triumphantly, 'King ...'", why does the model give "me" far more probability than "cat"?

- A. Because "me" is a more frequent word than "cat" in English as a whole
- B. Because the model stores "King me" as a fixed phrase and retrieves it
- C. Because the representation now carries that this is a checkers verb spoken by a winner
- D. Because "cat" is not one of the tokens in the model's vocabulary

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Raw frequency is what the model would fall back on with no context at all. Here the context has done its work: by the last stack the representation has been moved to a place where a winner in checkers is speaking, and that, not frequency, is what lifts "me."
- B. Not quite. That is the database picture, and the lecture rules it out. Nothing is stored as words to be retrieved; the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) is a fuzzy JPEG that nudges an embedding, and the probabilities are computed fresh from the contextualized representation.
- C. Correct. Attention moved "king" out of monarch-space using "board" and "triumphantly," and the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) added that this is checkers and a winner speaks. The probability for "me" is what that fully contextualized representation yields.
- D. Not quite. "Cat" is certainly in the vocabulary of any model trained on English. It receives a probability like every token; the point is that the probability is essentially zero, not that the token is missing.
