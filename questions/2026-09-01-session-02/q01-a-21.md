**Question:** In the Hurston exercise, every guess called out in the room was a noun. Why?

- A. The word follows "on," and the structure of the sentence demands a noun there
- B. Nouns are the most common words in English, so they are the safest guess
- C. Hurston's writing style leans heavily on nouns
- D. The slide showed a list of nouns to choose from

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. You applied syntax without thinking about it: after a preposition like 'on' comes a noun. A model has to learn that same structure, and it does so simply because guessing a verb there would be wrong every time.
- B. Not quite. Frequency alone would not stop someone guessing 'the' or 'is,' which are far more common than 'board.' It was the sentence's grammar, not word frequency, that ruled those out.
- C. Not quite. Style did come into the game, but at the last step, in choosing 'board' over 'deck.' Knowing the slot needed a noun at all came from grammar, before style entered.
- D. Not quite. No list was shown; the blank was open. Everyone converged on nouns from their own knowledge of how sentences work.
