**Question:** After the embeddings have passed through the final decoder stack, what does the model produce from the last position?

- A. The one correct next word, looked up from the vocabulary
- B. A probability for every token in the vocabulary
- C. A probability for only the tokens that fit the grammar
- D. A ranked list of its ten best candidate words

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Nothing in the model looks a word up, and nothing in it knows which word is correct. The output is a spread of probabilities across the whole vocabulary, and choosing one token is a separate step that comes afterward.
- B. Correct. The rich, contextualized representation at the last position is turned into a probability for each token the model knows, tens of thousands of them. Only after that spread exists does anything choose the next token, which is where temperature comes in.
- C. Not quite. Grammar does shape the probabilities, which is why a noun after "on" scores well. But the model does not first filter the vocabulary; it assigns every token a probability, and the ungrammatical ones simply get numbers near zero.
- D. Not quite. Some chat tools display a short list of alternatives, which makes this tempting. The model itself does not stop at ten; it produces a probability for every token in the vocabulary, and a top-ten view is just a slice of that.
