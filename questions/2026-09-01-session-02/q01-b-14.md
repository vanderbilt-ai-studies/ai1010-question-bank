**Question:** The lecture sums up token embeddings with the phrase "location is meaning." What does that phrase claim?

- A. Where a token sits in the embedding space is what it means to the model
- B. Where a word sits in a sentence determines what it means
- C. A word's meaning depends on where in the document it first appeared
- D. The model stores each word's meaning at a fixed address in memory

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The model has no dictionary. A token's meaning is entirely its position: tokens with similar meanings are placed near each other, and moving through the space moves through meaning.
- B. Not quite. Word order does matter later, when the surrounding words act on a token. But "location" here refers to position in the semantic space, not position in the sentence.
- C. Not quite. Position in the document is not the location in question. The embedding places the token in an abstract space of meanings, regardless of where it showed up on the page.
- D. Not quite. That would be a database, and the embedding space is not one. Nothing is stored at the location; the coordinates themselves are the meaning.
