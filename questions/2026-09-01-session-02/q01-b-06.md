**Question:** How does a model's vocabulary handle common words differently from rare ones?

- A. Frequent words get one token each; rarer words are built from pieces shared with other words
- B. Frequent words are dropped as filler, and only rarer words are given tokens of their own
- C. Every word gets exactly one token of its own, whether it is common or rare
- D. Rarer words are swapped for a common synonym before the model ever sees them

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Frequent words earn a dedicated token because they show up so often. Rare words are built from shared pieces, which is also what lets the model piece together a word it has never met.
- B. Not quite. Dropping common words is a habit from older keyword search, not from language models. The model needs "the" and "on" as much as any other token, because grammar lives in them.
- C. Not quite. A one-token-per-word scheme would need a vocabulary entry for every possible word, including misspellings and new coinages. Splitting rare words into shared pieces avoids that.
- D. Not quite. There is no synonym lookup. The rare word is kept, just broken into fragments the vocabulary already contains, so its exact spelling still reaches the model.

</details>
