**Question:** Training a language model by hiding the next word and having the model guess it is called self-supervised learning. What makes it "self-supervised"?

- A. The model checks each guess against a database of facts
- B. People label every training sentence as good or bad for the model
- C. The text itself supplies the answer, because the hidden word is right there
- D. The model writes its own rules of grammar and then follows them

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. There is no database in training or in the model. The only thing a guess is compared with is the word that was actually there.
- B. Not quite. That would be ordinary supervised learning with human labels, and it could never scale to trillions of examples. Nobody labels the training text at all.
- C. Correct. The next word in the text is the correct answer, so the training data grades the model's guesses on its own. No human has to write anything down, which is why training can run on trillions of words.
- D. Not quite. Nobody writes grammar rules, not even the model. It learns grammatical structure as a side effect of guessing well, without ever stating a rule.
