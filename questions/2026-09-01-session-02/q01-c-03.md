**Question:** In "She looked across the board and said, triumphantly, 'King me,'" which earlier words does attention weight most heavily when it moves the meaning of "king"?

- A. "board" and "triumphantly"
- B. "She" and "said"
- C. "me", the word that follows
- D. "looked" and "across"

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Those two words are the ones that tell you this is a game and someone is winning, so attention gives them the most weight in the average it builds for "king". The result lands "king" among board games rather than monarchs.
- B. Not quite. The subject and the speech verb tell you someone is talking, but nothing about what kind of "king" this is. Attention weights words by how much they matter to the token in question, and these matter little.
- C. Not quite. "me" does help settle the meaning once you read the whole sentence, but attention for "king" looks back over the tokens before it, not after. It has to do its work with "board" and "triumphantly", which is enough.
- D. Not quite. These sit right next to "board" and it is easy to lump them together, but on their own they could describe looking across a room or a street. The word that carries the game meaning is "board", and the word that carries the winning is "triumphantly".
