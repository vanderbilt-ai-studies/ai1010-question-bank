**Question:** The lecture asked whether JPEG is the format you would choose for archiving a photograph, and answered no. What does that answer imply about the MLP (multi-layer perceptron)?

- A. It should be replaced by a proper database as models improve
- B. You should not expect it to preserve every detail it was trained on exactly
- C. It compresses your prompt before reading it, losing some of what you wrote
- D. It keeps a low-resolution copy of each conversation it has had

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. The lossy, compressed form is not a defect to be engineered away; it is how a neural network holds what it learned. Larger models make the picture sharper, but it stays a picture, not a database.
- B. Correct. Archiving demands a lossless copy, and a JPEG throws detail away to save space. The MLP does the same with training text, which is why it can be confidently vague on specifics.
- C. Not quite. Your prompt is not compressed; attention can draw on all of it. The compression happened long before, when training text was squeezed into the weights.
- D. Not quite. The MLP does not change between conversations, and it stores nothing from them. What is low-resolution is its picture of the training data, fixed once training ended.
