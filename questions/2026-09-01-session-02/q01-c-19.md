**Question:** A classmate says, "When I asked for the capital of Australia, the model looked it up in its memory." What is wrong with that picture of the MLP (multi-layer perceptron)?

- A. Nothing; that is exactly how the MLP works when it answers
- B. It was attention, not the MLP, that did the lookup in memory
- C. There is nothing to look up: the MLP is weights, not stored facts
- D. The fact lives in the token embeddings rather than in the MLP

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Looking up implies a stored record to find, and the MLP has none. Its weights nudge an embedding toward "Canberra" because training text about Australia shaped them that way, which is a very different thing from retrieval.
- B. Not quite. Attention draws on what is in the context, and the capital was not in the prompt. But attention does not look things up either; nothing in the model does. The answer came from the MLP's compressed picture of the world.
- C. Correct. The MLP is a set of weight matrices, and the fact emerges from them as a nudge on the embedding, not as a record read back. That is why the answer is reliable for well-covered facts and shaky for rare ones.
- D. Not quite. An embedding is one word's location in semantic space, a first guess at meaning. Connecting a country to its capital is world knowledge, and that is what the MLP holds.
