**Question:** A student writes to a chat model, "My roommate's cooking is dangerously Vanderbiltian," and the reply treats the invented word as a joke about Vanderbilt. Which idea from the lecture best explains why the model coped?

- A. The model has a token for every proper noun, including newly coined ones
- B. The model autocorrected the word to the nearest real word before reading it
- C. The model memorized the word from a Vanderbilt web page in its training data
- D. Tokenization split the word into familiar pieces the model already knows

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. A vocabulary with an entry for every coinage would have to be infinite. The model manages without one by building unfamiliar words from parts.
- B. Not quite. Autocorrect lives in your keyboard, not in the model. The invented word reaches the model intact, just split into pieces.
- C. Not quite. The word was invented on the spot, so it could not have been memorized. What the model does have is meaning for the fragments "Vanderbilt" and "-ian" from other words.
- D. Correct. A rare or unseen word is broken into sub-word fragments that occur in many other words. The model can make sense of the parts, and so of the whole, even though it never saw the combination.
