**Question:** Hurston's actual word was "board." Why did the lecture single out that guess as demanding more than the others?

- A. Because "board" is the only noun that fits grammatically after "on"
- B. Because "board" has more meanings than any of the other guesses
- C. Because "board" was the only guess that had anything to do with ships
- D. Because reaching "board" takes a sense of style and world knowledge

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Syntax allows 'deck,' 'shore,' and 'star' just as well. What set 'board' apart was not grammar but the kind of knowledge it took to reach it.
- B. Not quite. How many meanings a word has was not the point. 'Board' stood out for being the choice a particular writer would make, not for being ambiguous.
- C. Not quite. Every guess was about ships or wishing: deck, shore, sail, star. Semantics got everyone that far; it did not separate 'board' from the rest.
- D. Correct. 'Board' is the more poetic choice, and it fits Hurston. To land on it you need to know something about how authors write and about the world, the third kind of knowledge a model has to learn.

</details>
