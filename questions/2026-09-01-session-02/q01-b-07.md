**Question:** Why can a language model make sense of a word that never appeared anywhere in its training data?

- A. It looks the word up online before responding
- B. Its vocabulary is large enough to contain every possible word
- C. The word is split into pieces that also occur inside other words
- D. It ignores the unfamiliar word and works from the rest of the sentence

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Nothing leaves the model between tokens. The ability to handle a new word comes from how the text was chopped up on the way in, not from any outside lookup.
- B. Not quite. No fixed vocabulary can hold every word people will ever coin or misspell. The model gets around that by not needing one entry per word.
- C. Correct. Rare and unseen words are tokenized as fragments shared across many familiar words. The model has meaning for the pieces, so it can assemble a reasonable reading of the whole.
- D. Not quite. Surrounding words help, but the new word is not skipped. Its fragments carry meaning of their own, which is why a made-up word can still be understood rather than dropped.
