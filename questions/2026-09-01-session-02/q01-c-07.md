**Question:** How far can attention reach when it gathers information to shape a token's meaning?

- A. Over everything in front of the model, including documents you pasted in
- B. Over the sentence the token belongs to, stopping at the period
- C. Over the handful of words immediately before the token in question
- D. Over what the model absorbed about the world during training

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Attention looks back across the whole context, so a document you attach or a fact you stated earlier in the conversation can pull on the meaning of a word later on. That is why what you put in front of the model can override what it learned in training.
- B. Not quite. It is natural to think of disambiguation as a sentence-level job, but attention is not fenced in by punctuation. The whole context, including earlier turns and attachments, is available to it.
- C. Not quite. Nearby words often matter most and get heavy weight, which makes this tempting. But attention takes its weighted average over every earlier token, however far back it sits.
- D. Not quite. What was absorbed in training is the territory of the MLP (multi-layer perceptron). Attention does the opposite job: it draws on what is in front of the model right now.
