**Question:** The model is working on the word "crane" in "Wading through the marsh, the tall gray crane stretched its neck." Which words can attention draw on for that token?

- A. Every word in the sentence, since the model takes the sentence in as a whole
- B. Only the words that come before it in the context
- C. Only the handful of words sitting closest to it
- D. Only the words the model has produced so far in its own reply

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. The whole sentence is in the context, but attention for a given token averages over the tokens that came before it. "Neck" has not been reached yet when "crane" is being resolved.
- B. Correct. Attention looks back: for each token it takes a weighted average of the tokens already in front of it. That is why "wading", "marsh", and "gray" can move "crane" toward the bird and "neck" cannot.
- C. Not quite. Nearby words often carry the most weight, but nothing fences attention into a window. It reaches back over the whole context, including a document you attached earlier.
- D. Not quite. The model's own output does join the context as it goes, but so does everything you supplied. For a token in your sentence, attention draws on the earlier words of that sentence.
