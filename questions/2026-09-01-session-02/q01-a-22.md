**Question:** A classmate says: "The model never makes grammar mistakes, so someone must have programmed the rules of grammar into it." Which idea from the lecture corrects this?

- A. Engineers add a grammar-checking step after the model produces its text
- B. A grammar textbook in the training data is where the rules come from
- C. Grammar was learned, not programmed, because guessing well required it
- D. The model copies grammatical sentences from its training text

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. There is no separate grammar checker. The pipeline ends with a token being chosen from probabilities; nothing edits it afterward.
- B. Not quite. Even if a grammar book were in the training text, it would be treated like any other text: more next words to guess. The model does not read rules and apply them.
- C. Correct. To predict the next word you have to know which kinds of word can go where, so grammatical structure falls out of the training itself. Nobody supplied a single rule, just as nobody in the room needed a stated rule to know a noun follows 'on'.
- D. Not quite. The model does not store or copy sentences; it has no text inside it. It produces each token from probabilities shaped by training, which is why it can write sentences that never appeared anywhere.
