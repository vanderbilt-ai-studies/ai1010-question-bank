**Question:** A friend tells you that ChatGPT must run on a brand-new architecture invented in the 2020s, since chatbots did not work well before then. Which point from the lecture answers this?

- A. Chatbots became useful once expert-system rules were finally written well enough
- B. The architecture dates from 2017 and has been scaled up rather than replaced
- C. Deep learning itself only began in the 2020s, so nothing older could have worked
- D. The architecture is the same but was rewritten to run on much faster computers

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Expert systems belong to an earlier era, and nothing in a modern chat model is hand-written rules. The improvement came from scale, not from better rule-writing.
- B. Correct. The transformer of 2017 is still the design in use. What made chatbots work well was training that same architecture on trillions of words instead of a million, and making the model much larger.
- C. Not quite. Deep learning dates from around 2010 on the lecture's timeline, with convolutional and recurrent networks. The transformer came in 2017, so both are older than your friend thinks.
- D. Not quite. Faster hardware helps, but the lecture attributed the leap to scale: bigger models and vastly more training text. Same design, more of it.
