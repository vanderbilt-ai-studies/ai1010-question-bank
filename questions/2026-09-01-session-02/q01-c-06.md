**Question:** Besides which earlier words matter most, what else is the attention layer sensitive to when it builds its weighted average?

- A. How often each word appeared in training
- B. How many times each word is repeated in the context
- C. The order the words came in
- D. How many decoder stacks the model has

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Training frequency shapes what the model knows, which lives in the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) and the embeddings, but it is not something attention weighs in the moment. Attention responds to the words in the context and how they are arranged.
- B. Not quite. Repetition is not what the weighting measures. Attention weights a word by how much it matters to the token being resolved, and a word that appears once can matter far more than one that appears five times.
- C. Correct. The same words in a different order can mean something different, and the grammatical slot "king" sits in is part of what told the room it was a verb. Attention keeps track of order as it averages.
- D. Not quite. The number of stacks is what makes a model large or small, and it changes how much refining happens overall. It is not an input to any single attention layer.
