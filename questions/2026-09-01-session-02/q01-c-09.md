**Question:** Nobody loaded a list of facts into the model, yet it can tell you the capital of Australia. Why does training on next-word guessing alone leave the model knowing things about the world?

- A. Because a fact sheet is assembled from the training text and stored alongside the weights
- B. Because the model was given a reference work to read before its ordinary training began
- C. Because you cannot guess the next word well without absorbing what the text is about
- D. Because the model keeps the sentences it guessed correctly and reuses them later

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. No fact sheet is built at any point. Training only ever compares a guess with the word that was actually there and adjusts the parameters.
- B. Not quite. There is no reading list and no first stage of that kind. Every sentence in training is handled the same way: hide the next word, guess, adjust.
- C. Correct. Text about Australia can only be continued sensibly by something that has picked up what is said about Australia. Facts, grammar, and style are all learned as a side effect of being scored on the next word.
- D. Not quite. Nothing is kept. A correct guess produces no record and no stored sentence, only a set of parameters that were already good enough to leave alone.
