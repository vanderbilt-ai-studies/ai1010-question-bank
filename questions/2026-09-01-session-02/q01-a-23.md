**Question:** The lecture pointed out what the next-word game was and was not asking of you. What was it asking for?

- A. The one true statement about ships
- B. A plausible continuation of the sentence
- C. The most common English word that can follow "on"
- D. The exact word from the novel, or nothing

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Nothing in the exercise checked whether the completed sentence was true. It was a line of fiction, and a good guess did not need to be a fact about ships at all.
- B. Correct. You were rewarded for a guess that fits, and 'deck' would have been a fine one even though Hurston wrote 'board'. That is the same deal the model is trained on, which is why what it produces is always plausible and never guaranteed to be correct.
- C. Not quite. Common words like 'the' can follow 'on,' but nobody guessed them, because the game rewards a good fit, not a frequent word.
- D. Not quite. Landing on 'board' was satisfying, but the exercise counted every sensible noun as playing the game well. It was about guessing, not about matching.
