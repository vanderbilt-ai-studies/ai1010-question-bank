**Question:** In the phrase "tools plus autonomy equals an agent," what does autonomy mean?

- A. The model decides for itself which tools it is given
- B. The model can decline tasks it decides are inappropriate
- C. The model has the freedom to loop until the job is done
- D. The model runs without any context supplied by the user

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. The tools come from the harness built around the model; the model chooses which of them to call, not which exist. Autonomy is about being allowed to keep working, not about supplying its own tools.
- B. Not quite. Refusing is a separate matter from autonomy in the lecture's sense. Autonomy here is the freedom to keep going, not the freedom to say no.
- C. Correct. A chat model produces one response and stops; an agent can think, write code, call a tool, look at the result, and go again as many times as the task needs. That loop, combined with tools to act through, is what makes it an agent.
- D. Not quite. Agents depend on context more than chats do: a problem statement, requirements, papers, and guidance all go in. Autonomy is not the absence of instructions but the freedom to keep looping on them until the job is done.
