**Question:** The token "king" has one embedding whether it appears in "the king of France" or in "King me." What does that tell you about what the embedding can and cannot capture?

- A. It captures everything about the word, so both sentences are read the same way
- B. It captures the sentence's grammar but not the word's meaning
- C. It captures nothing useful until the other words have been considered
- D. It captures the word's usual meaning but not its meaning in this sentence

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. The sentences are not read the same way in the end, or the model could never handle the checkers sense. The embedding is where reading starts, not where it ends.
- B. Not quite. It is the reverse: the embedding is all about the word's usual meaning and knows nothing about the sentence it landed in.
- C. Not quite. It captures plenty: "king" starts among monarchs, which is right most of the time. What it cannot do is tell when this particular sentence is the exception.
- D. Correct. The embedding is a location chosen without looking at neighboring words, so it holds the typical meaning. Working out the meaning in context is the job of the decoder stacks that follow.
