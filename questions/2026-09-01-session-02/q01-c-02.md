**Question:** For a single token, what does the attention layer actually compute?

- A. A lookup of the token's meaning in the knowledge the model stored during training
- B. A copy of the embedding of the single earlier word that is most closely related to it
- C. A weighted average of every earlier token's embedding, weighting the ones that matter
- D. A small amount of random noise so the same prompt can give different answers

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Stored knowledge is the job of the MLP (multi-layer perceptron), and even there nothing is looked up. Attention does not consult knowledge at all; it works with the embeddings of the words already in the context.
- B. Not quite. Attention does not pick one word and copy it. It blends every earlier token, giving more weight to the ones that matter, so "board" and "triumphantly" both pull on "king" at once.
- C. Correct. That average is what lets the surrounding words move a token's meaning: "board" and "triumphantly" get heavy weight and drag "king" toward board games. The lecture's phrase for it is averaging the information to get you in about the right place.
- D. Not quite. Noise is added once, at the end, when a token is chosen from the probabilities, and it is controlled by temperature. Attention is deterministic: the same context always produces the same weighted average.
