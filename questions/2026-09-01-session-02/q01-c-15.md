**Question:** Where does the knowledge in the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) come from?

- A. From the documents you paste into the context
- B. From what the model absorbed during training
- C. From an internet search the model runs while answering
- D. From the token embeddings, which already hold the facts

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. What you paste is read through attention, not stored in the MLP. The MLP's contents were fixed when training ended, before you ever typed a prompt.
- B. Correct. The MLP's weights were set by trillions of next-word guesses over an enormous body of text. Everything it knows about checkers, or anything else, was picked up that way.
- C. Not quite. The model as described in the lecture does not search anything; it takes a context and produces one token. Knowledge that is not in the context has to come from the weights.
- D. Not quite. An embedding is a first guess at a single word's meaning, a location in semantic space. Knowing which game you say "king me" in is far more than a word's location, and that lives in the MLP.
