**Question:** A student sees that a chat model has a "200k limit" on how much it can hold in one conversation and asks what that number is counting. What is the answer?

- A. Words, so roughly 200,000 words of prompt and conversation
- B. Characters, including spaces and punctuation
- C. Tokens, the word and sub-word pieces the model works in
- D. Times the model can be run before the conversation resets

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Words are the natural guess, but the model never sees words as such. Many words become more than one token, so 200,000 tokens holds fewer than 200,000 words.
- B. Not quite. Characters are too fine a unit. The model's tokens are whole common words or multi-letter fragments, so a character count would overstate what fits.
- C. Correct. Token limits and token costs both count the same unit the model reads and produces. That is why the same text can cost more or less depending on how many pieces it breaks into.
- D. Not quite. The limit is about how much text the context can hold, not how many turns you get. Every run counts the tokens in the whole context, not the number of runs.
