**Question:** A physicist trains the same next-item guessing architecture on gravity-wave readings instead of English text. Based on the lecture, what should she expect?

- A. The model learns the structure of gravity-wave data the way it learns English
- B. The model fails, because the architecture was designed around words
- C. The model needs a rulebook of physics loaded in before it can start
- D. The model translates the readings into English sentences before learning from them

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The learner is general-purpose: hide the next item, guess, adjust. Feed it English and it learns English; feed it gravity-wave data and it learns about gravity waves. Nothing in the method is specific to language.
- B. Not quite. The architecture is not built around words; it is built around predicting the next item in a sequence, whatever the items are. Words are just one kind of sequence.
- C. Not quite. No rules are written down for English either. The whole point of self-supervised learning is that the data provides its own answer, so physics would be learned the same way.
- D. Not quite. Nothing gets translated. The readings become numbers the model predicts directly; there is no English step in between.
