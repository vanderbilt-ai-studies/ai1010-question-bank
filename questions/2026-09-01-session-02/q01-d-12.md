**Question:** What does the decoder transformer's mechanism actually select for when it produces its output?

- A. Output that has been checked against a store of verified facts
- B. Output that matches its training documents word for word
- C. Output that is the most cautious answer available
- D. Output that is a probable continuation of the context

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. There is no store of facts to check against; the MLP (multi-layer perceptron) is a fuzzy JPEG, not a database. No step in the pipeline verifies anything, which is why plausible and correct can come apart.
- B. Not quite. Training taught the model to guess the next token well, not to reproduce any document. Its output is a fresh continuation drawn from probabilities, and it can produce sentences that appear nowhere in the training text.
- C. Not quite. Nothing in the mechanism prefers caution; a confident-sounding wrong answer and a hedged right one are both just continuations. What is selected is the probable next token, whatever tone that carries.
- D. Correct. The model was trained to guess the next token well, and that is the only thing it ever does. Nothing in the mechanism selects for true, which is why the lecture insists on plausible rather than correct.
