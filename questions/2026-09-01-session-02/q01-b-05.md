**Question:** The lecture deliberately stops saying a model predicts the next "word" and starts saying it predicts the next "token." Why does that distinction matter?

- A. "Token" is simply the technical word for "word" inside a neural network
- B. The unit the model works in is the token, a whole word or a piece of one
- C. "Token" is the unit companies bill you in, while the model itself still reads words
- D. The model reads one character at a time, and "token" is the name for a character

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. If tokens and words were the same thing, the change would be cosmetic. It is not: a token can be a whole word or a fragment of one, and that difference matters for limits, costs, and unfamiliar words.
- B. Correct. Common words get a token to themselves, and rarer words are broken into pieces shared across many words. Everything the model does, including what it charges for, is counted in those units.
- C. Not quite. Tokens really are what you are billed for, which is what makes this sound right. But the billing follows the mechanism rather than replacing it: tokens are the units the model reads and writes, and the price is just counting them.
- D. Not quite. Tokens are larger than characters. A common word is one token, and a rare word is a handful of pieces, not a string of individual letters.
