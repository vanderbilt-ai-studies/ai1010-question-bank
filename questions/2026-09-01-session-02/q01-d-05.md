**Question:** In the lecture, what is the temperature setting?

- A. A little noise added before the next token is chosen from the probabilities
- B. A filter that removes implausible tokens before the choice is made
- C. A dial that changes how much of the context attention reads
- D. A setting for how many decoder stacks the embedding passes through

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Temperature is the small nudge applied at the very end, when one token is picked from the distribution. It is what lets the model take a slightly different path through the same probabilities each time.
- B. Not quite. Implausible tokens already have probabilities near zero, so nothing needs to filter them out. Temperature does the opposite of narrowing: it adds noise so that a less-than-top token sometimes gets chosen.
- C. Not quite. Attention always looks back over the whole context, and no setting shortens that. Temperature acts after every stack has finished, at the moment of choosing a token, not while meaning is being computed.
- D. Not quite. The number of decoder stacks is fixed by the model's architecture, and it is part of what makes a model large or small. Temperature touches none of that; it only affects the final choice of a token.
