**Question:** Two people give a model the same 30,000-token thinking budget. One runs it on a laptop with no GPU (graphics processing unit) at about 5 tokens per second; the other on a specialized inference chip at about 3,000. Why is a long budget practical for one of them and not the other?

- A. Hidden tokens are produced one at a time like any others, so the budget costs real time
- B. The laptop cannot store a budget that large, so it discards the extra tokens
- C. The chip produces the thinking tokens in parallel, unlike the laptop
- D. The laptop is running a smaller model, which needs more tokens to reach an answer

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Thirty thousand tokens is about ten seconds at three thousand per second, and roughly a hundred minutes at five. A thinking token costs exactly what a visible one costs, so how much deliberation you can afford to wait for is decided by the hardware.
- B. Not quite. A budget is not something stored up in advance; it is only a ceiling on how many tokens the model may produce for itself. The laptop will happily produce all of them -- you simply wait a very long time for them.
- C. Not quite. Parallel processing belongs to the initial context, which arrives all at once. Generation is sequential on any hardware: each token has to be produced and fed back in before the next one can be chosen.
- D. Not quite. Nothing here says the models differ, and a smaller model would generate faster rather than slower. What separates these two is the speed of the hardware applied to a one-at-a-time process.
