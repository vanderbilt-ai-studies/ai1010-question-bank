**Question:** A student asks what a model's parameter count is actually counting. What is the answer?

- A. How many separate rules are written into the architecture
- B. How many individual numbers are stored in its weight matrices
- C. How many tokens the model can hold in its context at once
- D. How many training documents the model was shown

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. No rules are written in anywhere, so there is nothing of that kind to count. What can be counted is the numbers, and those are what the term refers to.
- B. Correct. Parameters are numbers, plainly -- the values filling all those matrices -- so the count is a broad measure of how much it takes to represent the model. It is a rough proxy for capability, not a precise one.
- C. Not quite. That is the context window, a separate quantity entirely. A model's parameters are fixed after training, while what fits in its context is about each individual conversation.
- D. Not quite. Training data volume is a different measure and is not what the number reports. Parameter count describes the model itself once training has finished.
