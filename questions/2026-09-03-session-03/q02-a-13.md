**Question:** You believe a quiz question in this course was wrong. What does the course let you do about it?

- A. File a challenge in Brightspace, and earn extra credit if it holds up.
- B. Nothing -- questions are checked by a second reviewer, so they are final.
- C. Ask for your own score to be corrected, which is all a challenge wins you.
- D. Raise it in class, where a class vote decides whether it stands.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Questions are drafted by a model and checked by a second one, and that process still lets errors through. A challenge that turns out to be correct is worth a point of extra credit.
- B. Not quite. The second check is real, but it was described as fallible rather than final. That is exactly why a route for challenges exists and why a correct one is rewarded.
- C. Not quite. You are thinking of it as a grade dispute over one attempt. It is treated as a contribution instead: a challenge that turns out to be right earns a point of extra credit on top of any correction.
- D. Not quite. Nothing about question correctness is put to a vote. A challenge is judged on whether it is actually right, not on how many people agree.
