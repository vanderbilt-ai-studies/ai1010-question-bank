**Question:** Which statement describes the difference between a reasoning model and a non-reasoning one?

- A. The reasoning model is trained on a curated set of logic problems rather than general text
- B. The reasoning model applies rules of inference that were programmed into it
- C. The reasoning model is larger, and size is what makes reasoning possible
- D. The reasoning model can decide a request needs more work and generate tokens first

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Nothing in the lecture ties reasoning to a special diet of logic puzzles. The distinction it drew was about what happens when you send a request, not about the training corpus.
- B. Not quite. No rules are programmed into any model in this family; whatever it does with language was learned from a signal. Reasoning here means generating hidden tokens, not executing logic that someone wrote.
- C. Not quite. Size and thinking are separate dials -- a smaller model can think and a large one can answer straight away. Confusing the two makes it impossible to reason about either.
- D. Correct. That decision, and the hidden stretch it opens, is the whole distinction. A non-reasoning model goes straight into the answer you can see.
