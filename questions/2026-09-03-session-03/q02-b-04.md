**Question:** You ask a reasoning model a hard question and it sits there for fifteen seconds with nothing appearing on screen. Why is nothing showing up?

- A. The request is queued behind other users and has not started yet
- B. The model is comparing several finished drafts and choosing the best one
- C. The model has crashed and will time out shortly
- D. The model is generating tokens it is keeping to itself before it replies

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Queueing would look identical from your side, which is why the misreading is so easy. But the lecture's account of the pause is that work is already underway, not waiting to begin.
- B. Not quite. There is no bank of finished drafts to compare. The model produces a probability distribution over the next token and walks forward one token at a time, both while thinking and while answering.
- C. Not quite. This is the reading the lecture went out of its way to correct. A silent stretch is normal behaviour for a reasoning model, not a failure.
- D. Correct. Generation is happening the whole time; it is simply going into the model's own context rather than onto your screen. The visible answer starts once the model stops thinking.
