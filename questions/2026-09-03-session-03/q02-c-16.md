**Question:** The lecture compared a model on a long thinking budget to a new intern who does not like coming back for directions. What point was that making?

- A. A model needs training on your material before you can trust it with a task
- B. A model should be given as little context as possible
- C. A model will ask clarifying questions when your request is unclear
- D. Given time but unclear directions, it works hard on the wrong thing

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. The comparison was not about training at all -- the model already has its capability when you hand it the work. The problem being described happens after the task is handed over.
- B. Not quite. Less context is not the lesson; a well-described task usually needs more detail, not less. The intern goes wrong for want of direction, not from an excess of it.
- C. Not quite. That is precisely what the intern in the comparison does not do, and it is why the analogy works. You find out about the misunderstanding only when the finished work arrives.
- D. Correct. The long budget guarantees effort, not that the effort points where you wanted. This is why the practical move is to test with a small budget before you spend a large one.
