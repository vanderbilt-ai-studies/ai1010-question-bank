**Question:** Why does a large thinking budget only pay off when the task is described well?

- A. A vague task makes the model stop thinking early and return an error instead
- B. A vague task lets the model spend the budget going the wrong way
- C. Long budgets are only unlocked once a prompt is specific enough
- D. Thinking tokens are discarded unless the prompt is specific

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Vagueness does not stop the model; it will proceed confidently on whatever reading of your request it settled on. The failure is silent, which is what makes it costly.
- B. Correct. The budget buys work, not correctness, so an unclear target just means more work aimed off to one side. That is the wandering off in the wilderness the lecture described.
- C. Not quite. The setting is yours to choose regardless of how you worded the request -- nothing inspects your prompt first. The risk is what you get for the money, not whether you can spend it.
- D. Not quite. The thinking tokens become the model's own context either way and shape the answer that follows. When the task is vague, they shape it toward the wrong thing.
