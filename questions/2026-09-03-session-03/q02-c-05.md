**Question:** A model is partway through its reasoning when it reaches the ceiling of its thinking budget. What happens next?

- A. It starts a fresh thinking pass with a larger budget
- B. It returns an error and asks you to raise the setting
- C. It wraps up and replies using the reasoning it managed
- D. It keeps thinking, since the budget is only a suggestion

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Nothing raises the budget on the model's behalf -- that dial is yours. Raising it is something you do on the next attempt, after seeing what the first one produced.
- B. Not quite. Running out of budget is not a failure state; it is the normal way a budget ends. You still get an answer, which is why a too-small budget can be hard to notice.
- C. Correct. The ceiling works like the clock on a timed quiz: when the time is up you hand in whatever you have worked out so far. The reply still arrives, just built on reasoning that was cut short.
- D. Not quite. The budget is a hard ceiling on thinking tokens, not a hint. If it were advisory there would be no way to control what a request costs you or how long you wait.
