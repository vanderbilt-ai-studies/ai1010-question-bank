**Question:** In the lecture's spoken-out-loud demonstration, the presenter said "hold on" before listing possible answers, and "stop thinking" before he replied. Inside a reasoning model, what do those two phrases correspond to?

- A. The prompt you send and the answer you get back
- B. Turning the thinking setting on and then off again
- C. The think token and the stop-thinking token
- D. The first and last layers of the decoder stack

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Those are the visible ends of the exchange, and they look the same whether the model thinks or not. The demonstration was marking the two boundaries of the hidden stretch that sits in between.
- B. Not quite. A setting is something outside the model that governs how much thinking is allowed. The demonstration was acting out something the model produces for itself, part way through generating.
- C. Correct. Both are ordinary tokens the model generates: one opens the mode where the tokens are kept back, the other closes it and the visible answer begins. Everything between them was material the model wrote for its own use.
- D. Not quite. The stack runs top to bottom for every token, thinking or not, so its layers cannot mark where a stretch of thinking starts and stops. The boundaries are tokens, not layers.
