**Question:** How does the size of the model change the risk of handing a long thinking budget to a loosely described task?

- A. The risk disappears once the model is small enough to be fast
- B. Model size has no bearing on it
- C. Smaller models are more likely to wander off with the time
- D. Only the largest models wander, since they explore more widely

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Speed only means the model reaches the wrong destination sooner. Being fast does not help you if the direction was never right.
- B. Not quite. Reasoning quality is one of the things you give up as you go smaller, and that is exactly the faculty that keeps a long think on target. Size is part of the picture here.
- C. Correct. The wandering risk is worse with smaller models, because a weaker grip on the task compounds over a long stretch of unsupervised reasoning. Check the direction early before you buy more of it.
- D. Not quite. Larger models are generally better at staying on a poorly specified task, not worse. Exploring widely is not the same as losing the thread.

</details>
