**Question:** A model tier is the same architecture built bigger. What is bigger inside the larger tier?

- A. A wider MLP (multi-layer perceptron), a wider embedding space, more stacks
- B. A larger vocabulary of tokens, so it has more words to choose between
- C. A longer conversation, so you can hand it more text in one sitting
- D. A richer set of tools and features built around the model itself

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Those are the three things you can widen, and each one is more numbers: bigger weight matrices in the multi-layer perceptron, more dimensions for a token's meaning to sit in, and more copies of all of it stacked up. That is why a bigger tier has more parameters and costs more compute on every token.
- B. Not quite. The vocabulary is the list of tokens the model can produce, and nothing ties its length to tier -- the probability distribution at the end covers the whole vocabulary either way. Growing a model means enlarging the parts that do the work, not lengthening that list.
- C. Not quite. How much you can put in front of a model is a property of the conversation, not of the model's size, and the two are set separately. Size is about the model's own numbers, which are fixed once training has finished.
- D. Not quite. Tooling is the part that mostly carries over when you drop a tier, which is why going smaller costs less than people expect. What a larger tier buys is more numbers inside the model itself.
