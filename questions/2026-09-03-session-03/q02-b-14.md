**Question:** A classmate explains a reasoning model this way: "It writes three answers behind the scenes, grades them, and shows you the best one." Where does this go wrong?

- A. It is right about the drafts but wrong that you never see them
- B. There are no parallel answers and no grader -- just one sequence of tokens
- C. It grades them, but on speed rather than quality
- D. It is right in outline, but the number of drafts depends on the model tier

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. The drafts are the invented part. There is no set of parallel answers, so there is nothing being hidden from you except the single stream of thinking tokens.
- B. Correct. The model only ever holds a probability distribution over the next token and steps forward. What looks like weighing options is that single sequence, written where you cannot see it.
- C. Not quite. Accepting the grading step is the error. Nothing in the model scores whole responses against each other, on any criterion.
- D. Not quite. Changing the count leaves the false picture intact. No tier produces multiple candidate responses and picks between them.
