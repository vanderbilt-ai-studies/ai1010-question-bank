**Question:** Why did the lecture argue that finding the smallest model which can do your work correctly is the responsible default?

- A. Smaller models hallucinate less often than larger ones do.
- B. Larger models are trained on data smaller ones may not use.
- C. A larger model burns far more compute, at a real cost.
- D. Smaller models are the only ones that run without a network.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Nothing about being small makes a model more truthful -- if anything it knows less and has more room to be wrong. The argument for going small is about cost, not accuracy.
- B. Not quite. Training data was not the issue raised here. The reason to reach for the smallest workable tier is the computation, and therefore the energy, a bigger one spends on every answer.
- C. Correct. Every answer from a very large model is a great deal of computation, and that computation has an environmental cost. Picking the smallest tier that still gets the work right means you are not spending it for nothing.
- D. Not quite. Where a model runs is a separate question from which tier you pick in a hosted picker. The responsible-default argument rests on energy use, not on connectivity.
