**Question:** Your internship uses a different AI (artificial intelligence) vendor, whose model names you have never seen. How does what you learned about Claude's tiers help?

- A. It does not -- tier reasoning is specific to one vendor's own lineup.
- B. Assume their default model sits at the same rung as Claude's default.
- C. Assume the vendor's newest model name is also its most capable one.
- D. Work out which rung of the ladder you need, then find their name.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. The trade-off comes from building the same architecture at different sizes, so it holds wherever that architecture is used. Only the vocabulary is vendor-specific.
- B. Not quite. Anchoring on a default is tempting because it gives you a fixed point, but a default is a vendor's choice about what to hand you, not a place on the ladder. You would still have to work out how much capability the job needs.
- C. Not quite. Newest and largest are different claims -- a vendor can release a small, fast model most recently. Position on the size ladder is what predicts capability.
- D. Correct. Every lineup is a ladder from small and fast to large and capable, so the judgement you actually make -- how much capability this job needs -- carries over intact.
