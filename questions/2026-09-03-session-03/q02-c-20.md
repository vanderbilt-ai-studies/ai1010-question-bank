**Question:** A classmate says that turning the thinking budget up is just another way of moving to a bigger model. What is wrong with that?

- A. Nothing is wrong: the budget menu is another name for the tier menu
- B. The budget is the cheaper way to buy the capability a larger tier gives
- C. A larger tier does whatever a longer budget does, so the budget is redundant
- D. They are separate dials, and a longer budget can lift a model past what it managed before

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. These are two menus over two different things -- how capable the model is, and how much work it may do before it answers. Treat them as one control and you have a single adjustment where the interface hands you two.
- B. Not quite. Extra time cannot supply world knowledge that a smaller model never had, so the two are not interchangeable at any price. Time helps when a problem has to be worked through, not when it has to be known.
- C. Not quite. A capable model on a short budget can still be cut off part way through a problem that has to be reasoned out. Capability and amount of work are separate things to buy, which is why there are separate dials for them.
- D. Correct. A Sonnet-sized model with a long budget may solve what the same model with a short budget could not, with no change of tier at all. The question to ask is whether the model can do the task at all or whether it just needs more time, and that split is less clean than you would expect.
