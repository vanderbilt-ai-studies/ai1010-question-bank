**Question:** Which comparison did the lecture use for what happens when a model reaches the end of its thinking budget?

- A. A fuzzy photograph of the world, losing detail as it is made smaller
- B. The clock on the in-class quiz, which cut thinking off
- C. An intern who never comes back for directions
- D. A dial that adds noise to a choice

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. That image is about model size -- a smaller model as a lower-quality compression of the world. It says nothing about how long a model may deliberate.
- B. Correct. The class was quite literally given a budget for thinking, and when the time was up, thinking stopped and answers were handed in. A model hitting its token ceiling does the same thing.
- C. Not quite. That comparison is about what goes wrong when a long budget meets a badly described task, not about what a budget is. It explains a risk rather than the mechanism.
- D. Not quite. That describes temperature, which nudges the model toward lower-probability tokens. Effort is about quantity of thinking, not about randomness in picking.
