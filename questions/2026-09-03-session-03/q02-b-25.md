**Question:** Which phrase best describes what thinking accomplishes for a reasoning model?

- A. A way to verify its answer before committing to it
- B. A way to produce useful tokens before it responds
- C. A way to spend more compute so the probabilities come out sharper
- D. A way to reduce the chance of picking a low-probability token by mistake

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Verification implies a check against something authoritative, and there is nothing of the kind inside the model. Whatever it writes while thinking is more generated text, not evidence.
- B. Correct. That is the whole mechanism in one line. The tokens go into its own context, and the answer is generated with them present.
- C. Not quite. The probability distribution for a given context is what it is; extra time does not sharpen it. What thinking changes is the context those probabilities are computed from.
- D. Not quite. How a token is chosen from the distribution is a separate control entirely. Thinking changes the material the model is working from, not the selection step.
