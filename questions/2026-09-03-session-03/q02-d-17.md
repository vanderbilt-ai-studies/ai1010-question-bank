**Question:** Having shown the pseudocode, the lecture made a point about size. What is genuinely enormous about a model like Opus?

- A. The volume of code, which runs to millions of lines
- B. The number of distinct rules the architecture states
- C. The length of the attention routine, which dominates the listing
- D. The weights -- on the order of a trillion numbers

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. The striking thing about the slide is how little there is -- attention alone is roughly five lines. Size lives somewhere other than the code.
- B. Not quite. The architecture states no rules about language at all; that is what makes it different from earlier approaches. Whatever the model represents was learned, and it lives in numbers rather than statements.
- C. Not quite. Attention is about five lines -- among the shortest parts of the slide, not the longest. Its compactness is exactly the surprise.
- D. Correct. The symbols in the pseudocode stand for matrices, and at this scale those matrices hold roughly a trillion values. Very little code, an unimaginable quantity of numbers, is a fair one-line summary of the whole family.
