**Question:** You type a one-line request -- "tidy up my project" -- with effort set to Max. A long wait later you get back a careful, thoroughly reasoned piece of work aimed at something you never asked for. Which idea from the lecture explains this best?

- A. The temperature was too high, so unlikely tokens crept in
- B. The model ran past its budget and answered with partial thinking
- C. A large budget on a vague request buys time to go the wrong way
- D. The model tier was too small for a request of this kind

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. High temperature produces varied or odd choices, not a coherent long effort pointed at the wrong goal. What you got was well reasoned -- just about the wrong task.
- B. Not quite. Hitting the ceiling gives you an answer built on unfinished reasoning, usually shallow rather than elaborate. Here the model had plenty of budget; the trouble was where it spent it.
- C. Correct. The budget guarantees work, not that the work is aimed where you wanted -- this is the intern who never comes back for directions. Describe the task properly, or probe with a small budget first and check the direction.
- D. Not quite. A bigger model would likely have guessed your intent better, but the root problem is that you never said what you meant. Moving up a tier is an expensive way to avoid writing a clearer request.
