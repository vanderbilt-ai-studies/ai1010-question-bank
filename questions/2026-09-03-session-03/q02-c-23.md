**Question:** You give a small model a carefully specified logic puzzle with the budget at Low, and the answer is wrong. No larger model is available to you. What does the lecture suggest is worth trying next?

- A. Raise the temperature so it explores more possible answers
- B. Give the same model a longer thinking budget
- C. Turn thinking off so it answers straight from world knowledge
- D. Shorten the prompt so less of the budget goes on reading it

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Higher temperature gives you a different answer, not a better-worked one, and on a puzzle with a single right answer that mostly adds noise. Exploration here needs reasoning steps, not randomness.
- B. Correct. The task is well described and mainly needs working through, which is exactly the case where extra time can substitute for capability. The same model with a longer budget may solve what it could not solve at Low.
- C. Not quite. World knowledge is the strength you lean on when an answer needs to be recalled rather than derived. A logic puzzle is the opposite case: it has to be worked out.
- D. Not quite. The thinking budget covers tokens the model generates for itself, not the tokens it reads from you. Cutting a well-specified prompt would remove the detail that keeps the reasoning on target.
