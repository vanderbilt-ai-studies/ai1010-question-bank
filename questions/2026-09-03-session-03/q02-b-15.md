**Question:** Before asking your question, you paste in your own rough notes working through the problem, and the answer you get back is noticeably better. Which idea from the lecture explains why that helps?

- A. You have supplied by hand the context a reasoning model writes for itself
- B. You have put the model into its thinking mode from the outside
- C. You have given the model more time to work before it has to answer
- D. You have narrowed the vocabulary the model draws its answer from

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Thinking helps because the answer is generated with extra relevant material already sitting in the context. It makes no difference to the mechanism whether the model wrote that material or you did.
- B. Not quite. The mode opens when the model produces a think token, and adding text to your prompt does not produce one. Your notes helped as context, which is a different route to a similar benefit.
- C. Not quite. Pasting text does not change how long the model may spend before responding. What changed is the material it is conditioning on, not the length of any budget.
- D. Not quite. The model still has a probability for every token in its vocabulary; context shifts those probabilities rather than removing options. The gain comes from what your notes give it to condition on.
