**Question:** Several companies are building chips specialized for the transformer architecture. On such a chip, a medium-sized model generates at a rate on the order of:

- A. Thousands of tokens per second
- B. Several hundred tokens per second
- C. Tens of tokens per second
- D. A few tokens per second

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. That is roughly two orders of magnitude above a hosted frontier model. It is what makes a long stretch of generation cheap in wall-clock time, which changes what is worth building on top of a medium-sized model.
- B. Not quite. This would only be a few times faster than a hosted frontier model, which would not change much. The whole reason these chips are interesting is that they clear the hosted rate by a much wider margin than that.
- C. Not quite. That is the range a hosted frontier model already occupies, so a chip that delivered it would offer nothing new. The specialized chips buy speed at the price of model size, and the speed gain is dramatic.
- D. Not quite. That is what you get on a laptop with no GPU (graphics processing unit) -- the slow end of the range, not the fast end. Purpose-built inference hardware sits at the opposite extreme.
