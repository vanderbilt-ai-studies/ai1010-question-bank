**Question:** Temperature is often described as affecting only the style of the final answer. Why is that description too narrow for a reasoning model?

- A. Thinking tokens are generated at a separate, higher temperature
- B. Temperature is applied once at the end, after the thinking has already been fixed
- C. It applies at every step, thinking included, so an early choice can redirect it
- D. Raising temperature also raises the thinking budget

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. There is no second temperature for the hidden phase -- the same setting is in force throughout. What matters is that the phase exists at all, so the setting has more to act on.
- B. Not quite. It is applied once per token, and the thinking is made of tokens too. Treating the reasoning as settled before temperature enters is exactly the misreading the question is aimed at.
- C. Correct. Thinking is ordinary next-token generation that happens to be hidden, so temperature is in force for all of it. One low-probability token early in the reasoning sends the model down a different path, and the answer follows that path.
- D. Not quite. The two dials are independent -- temperature never changes how many tokens the model may spend. What it changes is which tokens those are, at every step.
