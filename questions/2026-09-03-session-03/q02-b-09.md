**Question:** You ask a reasoning model a hard question. It sits silent for about forty seconds and then produces a three-sentence answer. A classmate says the timing makes no sense for so short a reply. What explains it?

- A. The interface held the finished answer back until the whole reply was ready
- B. The question was hard, so each token of the answer took longer to produce
- C. The model was choosing between several answers it had drafted
- D. Most of the tokens produced in those forty seconds were hidden thinking tokens

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. Buffering would mean the text already existed while you were waiting. During the silence your answer had not been started -- the model was producing other tokens first.
- B. Not quite. One token costs the same work whatever the question is; difficulty does not slow the pipeline down. Elapsed time tracks how many tokens were produced, and most of these were never shown to you.
- C. Not quite. There is no set of drafts to choose between, because the model only ever extends one sequence. Those forty seconds went into extending that single sequence out of sight.
- D. Correct. The length of the visible reply tells you nothing about how much was generated. Hidden tokens come out of the same pipeline one at a time, so they cost the same time each as the visible ones.
