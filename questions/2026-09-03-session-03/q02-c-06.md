**Question:** Why would you not just leave a model on its largest thinking budget for everything you do?

- A. Short, easy requests do not need thousands of thinking tokens
- B. A large budget makes the model less accurate on every task
- C. Budgets above Medium switch off the model's world knowledge
- D. The model refuses requests it judges too simple for the budget

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. A greeting or a one-line lookup gets no better for having ten thousand tokens of deliberation spent on it, and those tokens cost you time and usage. The budget should match the size of the problem.
- B. Not quite. More thinking is not accuracy poison -- on a hard, well-specified problem it genuinely helps. The trouble with a large budget is waste on easy tasks and wandering on vague ones.
- C. Not quite. Nothing about the thinking budget turns off what the model knows; the knowledge lives in the weights of whichever model you picked. Effort only governs how many tokens it may generate before replying.
- D. Not quite. The model does not police your setting -- it will happily think hard about a trivial question. That is precisely the waste you are trying to avoid.
