**Question:** While a reasoning model is in its thinking mode, where do the tokens it produces go?

- A. Into a log the vendor keeps for training the next version
- B. Into the model's own context, out of your view
- C. Into a scratch buffer that is cleared before the answer begins
- D. Into a rewritten version of your prompt, which replaces what you typed

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Hidden means not displayed to you, not shipped off somewhere else. Wherever else tokens might be recorded, the reason these ones matter is that they stay where the model itself can read them as it answers.
- B. Correct. That is precisely the difference between thinking and answering: same generation, different destination. Because they land in the context, the model can build on them when it responds.
- C. Not quite. If they were cleared, thinking would accomplish nothing -- the point is that the model has written itself context to answer from. They stay in the context; they are just not displayed.
- D. Not quite. Nothing edits your prompt on the way through; it sits in the context exactly as you sent it. The thinking tokens are added after it, which is how the model ends up with more to work from than you gave it.
