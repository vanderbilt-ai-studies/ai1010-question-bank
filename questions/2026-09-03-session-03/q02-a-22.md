**Question:** A classmate wants to reduce the environmental footprint of her AI (artificial intelligence) use but still get her work done. Following the lecture, what should she do?

- A. Try the smallest tier first and move up only when it falls short.
- B. Use the largest tier so she gets it right first time and avoids retries.
- C. Keep her chats short, since session length is where the cost sits.
- D. Pick whichever vendor's model name sounds smallest, whatever the work.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. This gets both halves: the work still has to come out right, and anything above the tier that achieves that is compute spent for nothing. Testing from the bottom finds that line.
- B. Not quite. It sounds efficient, but nothing about a larger model guarantees a first-time answer -- and the footprint tracks the size of the model that ran, not the number of tries. A big model that gets it right once has still spent a great deal of compute.
- C. Not quite. Chat length is not the main lever, and a short chat with an enormous model is still expensive. Which model is running is what drives the compute.
- D. Not quite. Names are labels a vendor chose, and they vary between lineups. What matters is the smallest rung that actually does your work correctly.

</details>
