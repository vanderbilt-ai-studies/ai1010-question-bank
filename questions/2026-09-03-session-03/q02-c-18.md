**Question:** You have a new task and no idea how much thinking it will need. What method did the lecture recommend?

- A. Start with a small budget, check the direction, then raise it
- B. Start at Max so you see the best possible answer, then bring it down afterwards
- C. Match the budget to how long you want the answer to be
- D. Pick one budget and keep it for every task

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. A small budget is a cheap probe: it tells you whether the model has understood the task before you pay for a long think. Once you can see it is on the right track, raising the budget buys real work rather than confident wandering.
- B. Not quite. Starting at the top spends the most usage on the attempt where you know the least about whether the task is well described. It also hides the problem, since a wrong answer arrives beautifully reasoned.
- C. Not quite. The budget covers hidden thinking, not the visible reply, so answer length is a poor guide to it. A short answer can need a great deal of working out.
- D. Not quite. A fixed setting is either wasteful on easy tasks or too tight on hard ones. The budget is a dial precisely because the right value depends on the problem.
