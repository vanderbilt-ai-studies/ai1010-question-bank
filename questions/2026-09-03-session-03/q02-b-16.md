**Question:** In the lecture's diagram of a reasoning model, the pipeline runs to token probabilities and then produces something in the slot where a plain next token would normally sit. What appears there?

- A. A blank placeholder token that stands in while the model deliberates
- B. A copy of the user's prompt, re-fed into the model
- C. A summary of the context so far
- D. A think token, opening the hidden mode

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: D**

- A. Not quite. There is no empty or placeholder token; every pass of the pipeline yields a real token drawn from the probability distribution. What appears here is an ordinary token that happens to open the hidden mode.
- B. Not quite. The prompt is already in the context and does not need resending. What comes out of the pipeline is always a newly produced token.
- C. Not quite. The model can certainly write a summary during its thinking, but that is many tokens of ordinary generation. The slot after the probabilities holds a single token.
- D. Correct. The diagram makes the point that thinking begins as an ordinary output of the pipeline -- one token, drawn from the probability distribution like any other.
