**Question:** When you move from a larger Claude tier down to a smaller one, what largely carries over with you?

- A. Most of the tooling the larger tier had
- B. Most of the world knowledge the larger tier had
- C. Most of the reasoning quality on hard problems
- D. Most of the wording of the answers it gave

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Size mostly buys knowledge and reasoning, not access. The smaller tier still reaches the same kinds of tools, which is why dropping a tier usually costs less than students expect.
- B. Not quite. World knowledge is one of the two things named as lost when you go smaller. A smaller model holds the world in fewer numbers, so the gaps show up here first.
- C. Not quite. Reasoning quality is usually the other casualty of going smaller. Some tasks will not survive the drop, which is why you test rather than assume.
- D. Not quite. Different tiers are different models, so they will not phrase things the same way even on an easy task. What carries across is the surrounding tooling, not the text.
