**Question:** You are brainstorming names for a student club, and every attempt comes back with the same safe, obvious suggestions. You want stranger ones. Which control changes this, and why?

- A. Temperature, since it lets lower-probability tokens be chosen
- B. Effort, since a larger budget widens the vocabulary the model draws on
- C. Effort, since more thinking tokens always produce more original wording
- D. Temperature, since it lengthens the model's thinking before it answers

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Safe and obvious is what you get when the model takes the top of the probability distribution every time. Turning temperature up flattens that preference at the moment of choice, so the odd option gets picked.
- B. Not quite. The vocabulary is fixed and the budget does not touch it. More thinking may give you better-considered names, but it does not make an unlikely word likelier to be selected.
- C. Not quite. Longer deliberation tends to produce more thorough answers, not more surprising ones, and it can converge on the same safe options more carefully. Novelty at the point of choice is a different mechanism.
- D. Not quite. You have named the right dial for the wrong reason, which matters because it will mislead you next time. Temperature acts in a single instant on the probabilities; it does not lengthen anything.
