**Question:** A classmate refuses to try Haiku for anything, saying a small model will not have access to the features he needs. What is wrong with his reasoning?

- A. Small models actually have more features, being simpler to extend.
- B. Tooling is largely shared across tiers; knowledge is not.
- C. Features depend on your subscription, so the tier makes no difference.
- D. He is right, so the smallest tier should be kept for casual chat.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. Nothing suggests smaller tiers get extra features. The point is narrower: the tooling is broadly the same, so the difference between tiers lies elsewhere.
- B. Correct. A smaller model keeps most of the same surroundings; it just holds less of the world and reasons less well on hard problems. Whether it suits a job depends on the job.
- C. Not quite. Your plan does affect what you can use, but the tiers are genuinely not interchangeable -- they differ in knowledge and reasoning. The claim to correct is the one about tooling.
- D. Not quite. That gives up too much. The real cost of a small tier is world knowledge and reasoning quality, and plenty of everyday work leans on neither.
