**Question:** A student asked how a model learns, given that it is not a brain. What did the lecture say actually happens?

- A. It predicts the next piece of the signal and is corrected -- backpropagation.
- B. Linguists write the rules of grammar into the model before training begins.
- C. It searches a stored collection of example sentences at the moment you ask it something.
- D. Its logic is designed by hand, then tuned by engineers across successive releases.

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. An architecture is designed, a capability is provided, and then the whole of training is that one instruction: predict the next piece of the signal, and be corrected. Nothing about language is stated in advance.
- B. Not quite. No logic and no rules are programmed in at any point. Whatever the model represents about grammar is worked out from the signal it was asked to predict, which is a genuinely different kind of system.
- C. Not quite. That describes a lookup system, not a trained model. There is no collection of sentences to consult -- there are weights, and they were arrived at by repeated correction during training.
- D. Not quite. Hand-designed logic is exactly what this family of models does without. Engineers design the architecture and the training setup; the content of what the model knows comes from the training procedure.
