**Question:** A team is about to train a model and decides to double the width of its embedding space, changing nothing else in the design. What have they done?

- A. Doubled how much text the finished model can hold in its context
- B. Made the finished model faster, since a wider space needs fewer layers
- C. Created room for more parameters, which training then has to fill
- D. Added knowledge to the model before any training has taken place

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: C**

- A. Not quite. Embedding width is how many dimensions each token's representation has, not how many tokens fit. Those are independent choices and this one does not touch the context window.
- B. Not quite. Width and depth are set separately -- widening one does not remove the other. A wider model has more numbers to multiply, so if anything it runs slower.
- C. Correct. Widths like this are hyperparameters, fixed before training starts, and raising one enlarges the matrices that hold the model's numbers. Those extra numbers arrive empty and only become useful once training has filled them, so the decision buys capacity, not capability.
- D. Not quite. Nothing the model knows is put in by hand at any stage. Enlarging the design creates space; only training, guessing what comes next and being corrected, puts anything into it.
