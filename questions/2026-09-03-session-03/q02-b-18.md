**Question:** Why should producing tokens that you never see make the visible answer any better?

- A. Because generating anything at all warms up the decoder stack for the real task
- B. Because the answer is conditioned on those tokens once they are in the context
- C. Because unseen tokens are generated at a lower temperature than visible ones
- D. Because the hidden pass identifies which facts the model is missing

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: B**

- A. Not quite. There is no warm-up effect; the stack performs identically on the first token and the thousandth. The benefit comes from what those tokens contain, not from the act of running.
- B. Correct. Every token is produced given everything before it, so material the model wrote for itself shapes what comes next. Thinking is a way to put useful tokens in front of the answer.
- C. Not quite. Settings do not flip between the hidden and visible halves of a response. The advantage of thinking is the content it leaves in the context.
- D. Not quite. Nothing audits the model for gaps. The hidden pass simply writes text, and that text helps because the answer is generated with it in view.

</details>
