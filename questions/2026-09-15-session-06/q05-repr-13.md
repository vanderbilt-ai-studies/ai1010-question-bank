**Question:** Why is circus a possible association with the trainer-and-cat sentence rather than a fact established by it?

- A. Learned associations suggest an unstated setting
- B. The token ID for cat explicitly names a circus setting
- C. Attention verifies that each suggested setting happened
- D. A changed representation proves that the setting is true

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. Learned associations can suggest a setting, but the sentence does not identify one. You should distinguish supplied evidence from an added interpretation.
- B. Not quite. An ID identifies a vocabulary entry. It does not encode a stated location, and the sentence itself leaves the setting open.
- C. Not quite. Attention uses information from context. It does not independently verify that a background association describes a real event.
- D. Not quite. A representation can incorporate plausible associations. The change itself does not supply evidence for an unstated fact.

</details>
