**Question:** Why should an automated grading procedure be compared with the rubric even when its score seems plausible?

- A. A plausible score can hide a mismatch in the procedure
- B. A plausible score makes the procedure the grading standard
- C. A reproducible score shows that every legitimate approach is covered
- D. A detailed explanation transfers grading responsibility to the skill

<details>
<summary>Show the answer and why each option is right or wrong</summary>

**Answer: A**

- A. Correct. The notes treat the grading skill as a draft procedure. Compare its rules with the rubric and the requested work; the instructor remains responsible for the final grade.
- B. Not quite. The procedure must be checked against the rubric and task. Its output does not replace those criteria or establish that its rules are appropriate.
- C. Not quite. A procedure can repeatedly apply a rule that misses a legitimate approach. Consistency does not establish that its criteria match the assignment.
- D. Not quite. An automated first pass supplies evidence to review. The instructor remains responsible for the final grade even when the explanation is detailed.
